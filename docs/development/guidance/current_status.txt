`scripts/analysis/aggregate_budget_fairness.py` (new, unit-tested in
`tests/test_aggregate_budget_fairness.py`) run against the completed

| method       | mean per-node best MSE | mean queries |
|--------------|------------------------|--------------|
| ace_env      | 0.1095 +/- 0.0453      | 483,460      |
| ace_student  | 0.0961 +/- 0.0182      | 130,140      |
| random       | 0.1923 +/- 0.0016      | 484,500      |
| round_robin  | 0.1928 +/- 0.0019      | 484,500      |
| max_variance | 0.1935 +/- 0.0012      | 497,100      |
| bayesian_oed | 0.1936 +/- 0.0021      | 484,200      |

Both ACE variants beat every baseline at matched total query cost:
`ace_env` by 43.1%, `ace_student` by 50.0% (while using 3.7x *fewer*
queries than the baselines it beats). Every individual ace_env seed
beats the pooled baseline mean, so this is not an averaging artifact.
Verified two data-quality questions before trusting this result:
  - `n_nodes` for ACE varies 28-30 across seeds (baselines are fixed at
    30) because some seeds' randomly-wired graph makes 1-2 nodes pure
    roots (no parents => no loss column in `node_losses.csv`), not a bug;
    per-node normalization over scored nodes is the same convention
    `scaling_common.py` already uses elsewhere in this paper.
  - ACE's best-episode MSE (0.110) and final-episode MSE (0.276) diverge
    substantially at this 40-episode budget (baselines' best/final match
    to ~1-2%). This is the known early-plateau-then-drift pattern, not
    new; reporting best-episode MSE is this paper's existing convention,
    but it is now flagged explicitly as a checkpoint-selection caveat in
    the paper's Budget Accounting section rather than left implicit.

## BLOCKING (PARTIALLY RESOLVED): budget-fairness at 5 nodes still pending

**Resolved at 30 nodes (see August 10 entry above): ACE wins under
total-query-matched accounting, by a wide and consistent margin. The
remaining open item is the 5-node rerun, which determines whether this
holds at the smaller scale too, not whether it holds at all.**

What was missing, precisely: Table 1 (and the 30-node table) in
`paper/iclr_ace_2027/paper.tex` use episode-matched accounting -- ACE and
the five baselines each run for N episodes, and the table reports final
loss at that episode count. This is exactly the comparison 4 of 5 NeurIPS
reviewers correctly objected to, because ACE's episodes are not
equivalent in oracle cost to the baselines' episodes (Appendix
`app:budget-extended`: ACE ~4-5x, Max-Variance ~65x, Bayesian OED ~500x
samples, Random/Round-Robin/PPO 1x, oracle queries per decision). Table 1
itself is unchanged (it remains the original executed-only protocol, as
stated in its own caption); the corrected, total-query-matched comparison
is reported separately in the Budget Accounting section instead of
replacing Table 1. **At 30 nodes, this has now been run and ACE wins**
(see the August 10 entry above). At 5 nodes it has not yet completed.

**What the remaining (5-node) experiment is, concretely** (30-node steps
1-4 below are DONE, see August 10 entry):
1. Pick a shared total-oracle-query budget (aggregate it from a completed
   run's `query_budget.json` via `scripts/analysis/aggregate_query_budget.py`;
   30-node used ACE env-lookahead's own mean total, 483,460).
2. Run ACE under `--lookahead_on_student` (1x multiplier, so its
   episode-budget and query-budget are the same number) AND under normal
   env-based lookahead with `--query_budget` set to the shared total.
3. Run the four adaptive/passive baselines (random, round_robin,
   max_variance, bayesian_oed; PPO excluded, already query-matched by
   construction since it never probes the oracle) with `--query_budget`
   set to the same shared total.
4. Compare best/final MSE across all methods at matched total queries via
   `scripts/analysis/aggregate_budget_fairness.py`. This is reported in
   the Budget Accounting section, alongside (not replacing) Table 1.
5. Job scripts: `jobs/curc_submit_5node_budget_fairness.sh`,
   `jobs/curc_submit_5node_budget_fairness_baselines.sh` (5-node, PENDING
   -- retrying after the OOM/memory-partition fix below);
   `jobs/curc_submit_30node_budget_fairness.sh`,
   `jobs/curc_submit_30node_budget_fairness_baselines.sh` (30-node, DONE).

**Why it matters / what depends on it:** the paper (Section "Budget
Accounting" and the Limitations section) commits to treating either
outcome as informative rather than picking one and hiding the other:
  - At 30 nodes, ACE wins under total-query-matched accounting under both

    episode-count and query-count accounting).
  - The 5-node outcome is still open. If ACE does not win there under
    total-query-matched accounting, the paper should say so explicitly

## ICLR 2027 Resubmission - In Progress

**Prior venue:** NeurIPS 2026 submission received critical reviews (4 of 5
reviewers flagged the same core issue: environment-query budget accounting
did not count lookahead probes, only executed interventions, making the
episode-count comparisons in Table 1 an apples-to-oranges comparison against
baselines that do not use lookahead). One reviewer (d6tT) also asked whether
DPO specifically was necessary, versus simpler preference-based updates.

**Decision:** Resubmit to ICLR 2027 rather than patch the NeurIPS version.
Reframe the paper's central claim around the LM-prior + lookahead-selection
mechanism (already the more defensible finding, per the anonymization and
ACE-w/o-DPO-tied-at-30-nodes results in the NeurIPS Section 5.2), with DPO
recast as one of several viable calibration layers rather than the headline
contribution. See the resubmission plan for full reasoning; do not edit the
plan file itself.
