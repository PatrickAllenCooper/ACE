## Title

From Experimental Histories to Causal Surrogates: Data Reuse, Optimization, and Error Propagation

## Abstract

A sequential experimental learner need not be the best predictor delivered after collection ends. We assess post-collection refitting of structural causal model (SCM) surrogates with known graphs and measured intermediates. A frozen prospective study evaluates fixed initialization zero on held-out root actions in twenty independent systems per family. Delivery/rolling NMSE ratios are 2.45956 in the five-node family and 0.274321 in the thirty-node family; delivery/flat ratios are 0.356194 and 0.391027, respectively. Both flat contrasts and the thirty-node rolling contrast meet the registered superiority rule; the five-node rolling comparison instead favors the rolling learner. On one fixed emulator, a completed confirmation across 12 histories gives a geometric delivery/online snapped-error ratio of 0.183 (95% interval 0.102–0.327), using the median of three scored refit initializations; 11 histories improve. Exploratory attribution on that exposed grid favors long fits on retained histories. An archived physical case study retains all eleven conditions: delivery beats rolling learning in 7 but Fourier regression in 0. Conditional eligibility, propagation and quantization results explain why local fit, joint support and downstream prediction must be distinguished. The evidence supports family- and comparator-dependent delivery benefits, with differing supervision and fitting compute; it establishes neither an architecture effect nor an acquisition or foundation-model advantage.
