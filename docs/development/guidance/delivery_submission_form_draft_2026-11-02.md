## Title

From Experimental Histories to Causal Surrogates: Data Reuse, Optimization, and Error Propagation

## Abstract

We assess post-collection refitting of structural causal model (SCM) surrogates with known graphs and measured intermediates. A prospective study uses twenty independent systems in each of two noise-disabled families, with five or thirty nodes. Its registered analysis weights balanced and matched-random collection histories equally within each system. At shared initialization zero, the pooled results favor delivery over a flat refit in both families and over rolling learning in the thirty-node family. Five-node delivery instead has 2.46 times the rolling error, a reversal whose cause remains undiagnosed. The flat comparison also depends strongly on collection history. For thirty-node balanced histories, the delivery/flat error ratio is 0.912 and delivery improves only 10 of 20 systems, compared with a ratio of 0.168 for matched-random histories. Across delivery, rolling and flat in the two families, median root-mean-square errors are about 0.5% to 3.4% of the training target's standard deviation. Separate archived studies favor long fits on retained histories of one emulator but find that Fourier regression beats delivery in all eleven physical conditions. Conditional theory explains why eligible-row fitting, joint support and prediction through a learned chain must be considered separately. These results support evaluating a final refit against the rolling learner and simple controls, with explicit attention to collection history, initialization and unequal training budgets.
