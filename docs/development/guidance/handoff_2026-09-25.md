## What this thread was actually about

The AISTATS 2027 draft (`paper/aistats_ace_2027/`) claimed ACE (an LM-guided
active-intervention policy for estimating structural causal mechanisms) beats
passive/Bayesian baselines by wide margins, headlined by "3× at 30 nodes."
**That headline, and every other ACE-vs-baseline number in every paper
version (NeurIPS 2026, ICLR 2027, AISTATS 2027), was invalid.** A metric
audit found four independent confounds; correcting them changes the paper's
central claim. What follows is the timeline of that discovery and everything
built in response.

## 1. The metric audit — four confounds, all fixed in code

Full record: `docs/development/guidance/metric_audit_2026-09-10.md`
(has an "Outcome" section with the corrected numbers and an "Addendum" for
confound 4). Do not cite any pre-audit number.

| # | Confound | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ACE's evaluator weighted root-node error ×0.2 and scored non-roots on broad-range parent contexts; the baselines' evaluator weighted roots ×1.0 on an observational sample. Never comparable. | Both evaluators ported to `baselines.py`; every runner logs both columns now. |
| 2 | At N≥15, `LargeScaleSCM.generate()` redrew mechanism coefficients on every call — baselines chased a moving target while ACE (via a separate adapter) ran on frozen coefficients. | Coefficients frozen at construction; `coeff_seed` reproduces ACE's exact draw. Verified bit-identical (`scripts/analysis/test_metric_parity.py`). |
| 3 | Different validation distributions for non-root mechanisms (broad-range vs observational). | Not correctable retroactively (no saved checkpoints); both scores now logged going forward. |
| 4 | **The student networks were never matched.** ACE's per-mechanism MLP is (64,64) trained 100 epochs/step; every baseline (in every paper version, and in the first audit re-run) used (16,) trained 50 epochs. A 16-unit ReLU cannot fit the 5-node benchmark's collider (`X3 = 0.5·X1 − X2 + sin(X2)`); this is most of the apparent ACE advantage. | `StudentSCM` takes `hidden_dims`; both runners default to `--student_arch ace --train_epochs 100`. |

**135 baseline cells were re-run** on confounds 1–3 (`results/audit_reruns/`,
commit `78dc0d2`) before confound 4 was found. **Corrected outcome, one
metric for every arm** (end-of-campaign non-root broad-range MSE per node,
mean over episodes — the student resets every episode, so this is min-free):

- **5-node, matched student:** ACE 0.165 ≈ round-robin 0.161 (p=0.85) — **a
  tie**, not the paper's headline win, once the student is matched.
- **≥15 nodes (LargeScaleSCM):** ACE-with-DPO loses to Random/round-robin at
  every scale tested (15/30/50 nodes), p≤0.01. ACE-without-DPO ties Random at
  15–30 and loses at 50. The repo's "Bayesian OED" baseline (a one-step
  *oracle* lookahead, not actually Bayesian OED) doesn't beat Random either.
- **DPO calibration hurts, monotonically, at every N≥15.**
- Passive baselines look converged under observational validation
  (~0.02/node) but are 4–8× worse and still drifting under broad-range
  validation — a real, reportable finding about validation-metric choice.

The pre-audit numbers are still sitting in `paper/aistats_ace_2027/paper.tex`
uncorrected except for one p-value edit (commit `3efc31c`, itself superseded
by the audit). **The paper needs a full reframe, not edits** — see §4.

## 2. PEV — a new method designed to survive at scale

Design doc: `docs/development/guidance/pev_design.md`. Once ≥15-node results
were established as dead for every existing method (including the
oracle-lookahead ones), the diagnosis was: nothing that costs oracle queries
to score candidates can win, and the benchmark family (`LargeScaleSCM`) is
too homogeneous for *any* targeting rule to beat Random.

**PEV (Propagated-Expected-Variance-reduction)**: a K=5 bootstrap ensemble
student (`baselines.EnsembleStudentSCM`, `EnsembleLearner`) plus an
acquisition (`PropagatedVariancePolicy`) that scores `do(X_j=v)` by the
expected reduction in integrated epistemic variance it would buy, over the
*evaluation* domain, computed via ensemble covariance — zero oracle queries.
A first, naive "go where variance is highest" version failed instructively
(chased irreducible disagreement on the collider, starved the quadratic
node); the reduction-based score fixed it. Both live in `baselines.py`.

Also built: `experiments/heterogeneous_scm.py` (a graph family with mixed
mechanism forms — linear/quadratic/sin/tanh/abs/product — since
`LargeScaleSCM`'s homogeneity is *why* nothing beats Random there; PEV needs
a benchmark where targeting can matter), and an LM-proposer ladder inside
ACE's own pipeline (`--proposer {random,heuristic}` in `ace_experiments.py`,
`jobs/curc_submit_proposer_ladder.sh`) to isolate whether the LM contributes
anything beyond the learner/lookahead/scaffolding it sits inside.

**Preliminary local result (Mac, CPU, 3 seeds × 40 episodes, matched
student — not yet confirmed at scale):**

| Arm | End-of-campaign non-root MSE |
|---|---|
| ACE | 0.165 |
| round-robin (matched student) | 0.161 |
| Random + ensemble student, no acquisition | 0.172 |
| **PEV** | **0.045** |

PEV beat same-student Random (p=0.012) and ACE (p≤0.001), and — unlike
ACE — improved *both* the collider and the quadratic node rather than
trading one for the other. This is the promising result the whole ladder
(item 1 above) is meant to confirm at real scale (10 seeds, 171 episodes,
30 nodes, the heterogeneous family, query-matched). **It has not been
confirmed on CURC. Treat it as encouraging, not decided.**

## 3. The OOM problem is NOT solved — full timeline

This is the main outstanding difficulty and cost real compute. Be skeptical
of any "fixed" claim here, including mine from 20 Sept — it was wrong, or at
best a partial mitigation.

**17–18 Sept**: A cluster-wide power outage killed several running jobs
(instant `FAILED`, exit 53) — unrelated to what follows, just context for why
some jobs were mid-retry.

**20 Sept — first diagnosis**: On resubmission, six GPU jobs
(`dpoalt_rank_s1011`, four `dpoalt_none_*`, `nodeimp_full_s42`) died with
SLURM state `OUT_OF_MEMORY` (host RAM via the cgroup `--mem` limit, confirmed
**not** a GPU/CUDA error) after 4–13 hours at 128G; a seventh
(`dpoalt_none_s123`) ran the full 48h wall-clock and hit `TIMEOUT` instead
(a separate, likely benign, "some seeds are just slow" issue — `--no_dpo`
doesn't touch early stopping, confirmed by grep). Ten more jobs
(`prop_random_*`, `prop_heuristic_*`, part of the new proposer ladder) were
still queued.

Static code reading found the plausible mechanism: candidate scoring
deep-copies the student network + optimizer + the *entire* replay buffer, up
to `num_candidates` times per step — thousands of times over a 200-episode
run — with tensor shapes that vary as the buffer fills across each episode.
PyTorch's CUDA caching allocator never returns freed blocks to the driver;
its own host-side bookkeeping grows with the number of distinct block sizes
it has seen. This is consistent with every observed fact (host-RAM-only,
multi-hour onset, seed-to-seed variance) and — critically — a local CPU
repro of the *exact same code path* showed no leak (bounded, spiky RSS,
6–14GB, plateauing), which is what you'd expect if the mechanism is
CUDA-allocator-specific and therefore invisible on CPU.

**Fix applied (commit `bcd017d`)**: `torch.cuda.empty_cache()` once per step,
`gc.collect()` every 10 episodes. Pure cleanup, no computation change.
**Recommended (and followed): canary-test one job before releasing the other
15 held ones.** `dpoalt_rank_s1011` resubmitted fresh with the fix at 128G.

**25 Sept — the fix did not work.** Checked via the `curc-access` skill
(newly available this session — see §5). Actual outcomes:

| Job | Result | Elapsed |
|---|---|---|
| `dpoalt_rank_s1011` (canary, has the fix, 128G) | **OUT_OF_MEMORY again** | 9:14:41 (vs 4:56:06 before the fix) |
| `nodeimp_full_s42/123/456`, `nodeimp_no_nod_s42/123/456` (6 jobs, have the fix, 128G — these were released from hold at some point without waiting for canary confirmation, by whom/when is unknown) | **All 6 OUT_OF_MEMORY** | 1:57 – 11:18 hours |
| `prop_random_*`, `prop_heuristic_*` (10 jobs, submitted 17 Sept with **64G baked into the original sbatch call** — my later 64G→128G fix to the submit script can't retroactively change an already-queued job's resource request) | 9 of 10 **OUT_OF_MEMORY**, 1 (`prop_random_s456`) completed | 19–61 minutes (much faster — consistent with half the memory ceiling) |
| `dpoalt_none_s123` | Still shows the original `TIMEOUT` (2 days) from before the fix; the other 4 `none` seeds were never resubmitted | — |

**Honest read of the evidence:** the empty_cache/gc.collect fix roughly
**doubled** survival time (4:56 → 9:14 for the identical canary seed) but did
**not** eliminate the growth — it's still climbing toward the ceiling, just
slower. That's actually informative: it confirms the CUDA-caching-allocator
mechanism is real and partially addressed, but something in the same family
is still leaking, or the mitigation's effect is too small relative to run
length. The proposer-ladder failures are a **separate, already-understood**
artifact (stale 64G resource request) and not new evidence either way.

**What to try next, in order of effort:**
1. **Instrument a real run.** Add periodic `torch.cuda.memory_allocated()` /
   `torch.cuda.memory_reserved()` / host RSS logging (e.g. via `psutil`,
   every N steps) directly into `ace_experiments.py`, submit one short GPU
   job, and get an actual growth curve instead of inferring from wall-clock
   failure times. This is the highest-leverage next step — everything so far
   has been indirect.
2. **Stop deep-copying the optimizer.** `SCMLearner.__init__` builds a fresh
   `optim.Adam` per candidate clone; Adam allocates per-parameter momentum/
   variance state on construction. Reusing one clone structure and resetting
   weights (instead of `copy.deepcopy` + new `SCMLearner` every candidate)
   would eliminate most of the allocator churn rather than just cleaning up
   after it.
3. **Try `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True`** (already used
   elsewhere in this repo for a different fragmentation issue — see
   `jobs/curc_large_scale_seed.sh`) on these jobs specifically.
4. If none of that resolves it, the blunt fallback is a much larger `--mem`
   (ah200 nodes may have more than 128G available — check with `sinfo -o
   "%N %m" -p ah200` — and just let the job "win the race" against a slower
   leak, which is not a fix but may be good enough to get calibration-suite
   numbers before the deadline).

**Do not resubmit the remaining GPU work (`none`-mode seeds, or a fresh
proposer-ladder run) until one of the above is tried.** Repeating the same
canary-and-hope cycle a third time without new information is how compute
gets wasted, not how the bug gets found.

## 4. The paper decision — Framing A vs B

Full plan: `docs/development/guidance/paper_reframe_2026-09-17.md`.

- **Framing A (method paper)**: PEV + the heterogeneous family + the audit's
  negative results as motivation. Requires the PEV ladder to clear
  pre-registered criteria (PEV beats same-student Random/round-robin on the
  hetero family at 30 nodes, p<0.05; doesn't lose on LargeScaleSCM; matches
  or beats ACE at 5 nodes at ≤1/4 the queries).
- **Framing B (evaluation paper)**: the audit itself — four confounds
  methodology, corrected comparisons, the observational-vs-interventional
  validation gap, "BOED is actually oracle lookahead," DPO-as-harm. Fallback
  if A doesn't clear its bar. Likely better suited to TMLR/CLeaR than AISTATS
  regardless.

**This decision cannot be made until the PEV ladder (§ "Do this first" #1)
has results.** Given it hasn't even been submitted yet and the deadline is 11
days out, **budget realistically**: ~2 days queue time + at least 1–2 days to
analyze + writing time. If the ladder isn't submitted within the next day or
two, Framing A is very likely off the table for the 6 Oct deadline purely on
time, independent of what the results show.
