# Next transfer test: sparse repair when localization is hard

Status: design only; no new target responses generated. This follows the fresh floor-six result, whose allocation and thresholds were selected after earlier development. It must not be counted as another confirmation of that result.

## What the existing evidence permits

The current 30-node, one-changed-node held-out-form experiment mostly gives away localization. On the 20 fresh systems at source_n=16, the strong change ranks first in 19 systems and second in one. The weak change ranks first in 18, third in one, and outside the logged top eight in one. At source_n=64, the strong change ranks first in all 20; the weak change ranks first in 17 and third/fourth in three. The floor-six policy therefore gives 11 responses to the changed node in 79 of 80 cells. These ranks and counts are read from the archived `actions.json` and `system.json` records, not a new simulation. The changed-node score improvement is unsurprising under that nomination rate. The soft mixture often matches scratch on the changed node, leaving no demonstrated reuse benefit.

## Scientific question

Can a modular source library lower target adaptation cost when **multiple** local mechanisms change and the initial assay may confuse a true mechanism change with noise or upstream distribution shift?

## Staged experiment

1. **Simulator gate first:** the existing `transfer_safe_switch_dev.target` and its descendants draw each node's two input coordinates independently. They contain no connected SCM and cannot produce an upstream distribution shift. Reuse the separate `connected_motif` bounded-degree DAG generator as a starting point, with structurally identified roots, explicit conditional mechanisms, interventions, and an oracle for noise-free conditional means. Under a source-to-target change at an upstream node, verify with common parent contexts that an unchanged descendant's conditional mean remains byte-identical while its observed parent distribution changes. Include an unaffected nondescendant. Reject the generator if either property fails. Before the full transfer screen, integrate this graph with the source-learning, held-out-form, and target-acquisition code and verify its conditional truth independently. Keep old independent-node results labeled as mechanism-wise transfer tests.
2. **Query contract:** the connected simulator produces one fully observed 30-node trajectory per query, and one action can supply natural labels for several motifs. The old independent-node floor-six plan's 200 *per-node* response budget is therefore invalid here; do not import that allocation rule into the connected experiment. Use four unmanipulated assay trajectories (cost four) followed by five batches of eight pair-intervention trajectories. Charge one unit per trajectory plus four per actuated node, yielding 72 per pair batch and **364 total cost**. All natural, unmanipulated motif labels from each trajectory enter every learner; manipulated child mechanisms are masked. Count source training trajectories and target trajectories once, report actuator uses and natural/masked motif labels separately, and store action-level ledgers. The assay is shared across policy arms. Comparisons should use the same total cost, sample count, and intervention menu.
3. **Development only:** after that contract passes, create 12 new 30-node connected systems with three changed mechanisms. Include one strong in-bank coefficient change, one weak held-out-form change, and unchanged descendants whose *parent distributions* shift due to the upstream change. Generate changed mechanisms from distinct hidden forms; serialize the graph and all conditional means. Keep source_n=16 and 64 as separate strata. Report the complete assay-rank distribution, top-four recall/precision, and false positives before comparing final prediction error. The untouched nodes must include both descendants and nondescendants of changes.
4. **Controls:** shared observational assay; fixed varied-value pair schedule, coverage pair, and adaptive pair selectors at 364 total cost; source-warm, scratch, and mixture estimators on each *identical acquired dataset*. A learned source representation receives a separate training-cost ledger. Compare acquisition effects within estimator and transfer effects within acquired data. Include the single-action menu only in a separately cost-matched comparison; a different number of acquired trajectories must be reported explicitly.
5. **Promotion decision:** freeze a new protocol on independent systems only if one development policy protects unchanged-node error and improves the changed-node adaptation curve against both the strongest fixed pair schedule and scratch on the same data. Require the portfolio's 20% primary gain and a paired system-level interval excluding zero on at least 20 fresh systems; require a prespecified 5% unchanged-node noninferiority interval. A point ratio alone is insufficient. Include both source-size strata, count simulator trajectories, actuator uses, and acquired natural motif labels separately, and report rank failures.
6. **If the numerical library provides no same-data benefit:** retain the sparse-change acquisition result as a distinct numerical study. Do not scale to a neural module library or claim a foundation-model transfer mechanism merely because active allocation helps.

The simulator gate and 12-system development screen are numerical work suited to local CPU. CURC becomes useful for a 1–5M parameter modular neural baseline or a larger independent corpus after the data and evaluation contracts are implemented and checked. No Azure or other closed-model call is part of this stage.
