  author format.key output

  new.block
  crossref missing$
    { journal emphasize "journal" output.check
      eid empty$
        { format.vol.num.pages output }
        { format.vol.num.eid output }
      if$
      format.date "year" output.check
    }

        { format.eid output }

\section{Conditions and limits of delivery}
\label{sec:theory}
We study a known acyclic graph with measured parents. The following statements
explain permissible reuse and possible error amplification. They do not prove
identification for arbitrary neural models or certify the fitted networks.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Mechanism eligibility]
Let $V_i=f_i(V_{\mathrm{Pa}(i)})+U_i$ be an invariant additive-noise
mechanism. In each experiment, suppose the current exogenous noises are
independent across mechanisms and independent of all past experiments; $U_i$
is integrable and has mean zero. An intervention policy may depend on previous
experiments, but must choose its action before the current exogenous noises are
realized. Retain a row only when the action does not replace mechanism $i$,
and let $S_i$ denote the final retained-row indicator, including any terminal
selection. Require $\mathbb E[U_i\mid V_{\mathrm{Pa}(i)},A,H,S_i=1]=0$
on the retained support, where $A$ is the current action and $H$ the preceding
history. Conditional independence of $S_i$ and $U_i$ given parents, action and
history is a sufficient retention condition. Then, in the retained mixture,
\[
 \mathbb{E}[V_i\mid V_{\mathrm{Pa}(i)}=p,\text{retained}]=f_i(p).
\]
Thus squared-loss regression can target this mechanism on supported parent
values, assuming finite second moments. This is an estimand statement; finite
data, optimization and extrapolation guarantees require further assumptions.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
In an acyclic graph, a node's parents depend only on earlier exogenous noises
and the chosen action. The current $U_i$ is independent of those noises and of
past experiments. Since the policy precedes the current realization, and the
stated retention condition preserves conditional mean zero. Taking the further
conditional expectation over action and history yields the assertion after
substitution of the structural equation.
The conditional expectation minimizes population squared loss on the retained
support. No statement outside that support follows.
\end{proof}

Confounding, temporal dependence of current noise on prior experiments,
missing parents, interventions that change rather than clamp a
mechanism, and response-dependent retention can invalidate the premise. In
particular, selecting the best probe using its measured response can change
the noise distribution. All-paid reuse avoids discarding probes on that basis,
but still requires invariant mechanisms and measured parents. The current
confirmation uses a deterministic emulator; this distinction matters for
any extension to noisy systems. Even within this additive-noise class,
composing conditional means need not produce a
downstream interventional mean through a nonlinear mechanism.
For example, clamp $W=0$, set $X=W+U$ with
$\Pr(U=1)=\Pr(U=-1)=1/2$, and let $Y=X^2$. The conditional-mean mechanism
for $X$ gives zero at $W=0$, so composing the mean mechanisms predicts zero
for $Y$. The actual interventional expectation is one. The zero-noise
structural map and the noisy interventional mean are distinct estimands,
even when both conditional-mean mechanisms are exactly known.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic DAG propagation]
Consider a deterministic SCM and its learned functions $\hat f_i$, evaluated
in topological order under identical correctly clamped interventions.
For each non-clamped node, assume
$|\hat f_i(v_{\mathrm{Pa}(i)})-f_i(v_{\mathrm{Pa}(i)})|\leq\epsilon_i$
at every true parent vector in the deployment support. Assume also
\[
 |\hat f_i(p)-\hat f_i(q)|\leq
 \sum_{j\in\mathrm{Pa}(i)}L_{ij}|p_j-q_j|
\]
on a region containing both true and predicted parent vectors, with
$L_{ij}\geq0$. Writing $e_i=|\hat v_i-v_i|$, one has
\[
 e_i\leq\epsilon_i+\sum_{j\in\mathrm{Pa}(i)}L_{ij}e_j.
\]
Correctly clamped nodes have $e_i=0$. If their rows in $L$ and their local
bounds are set to zero, the vector bound is
$e\leq(I-L)^{-1}\epsilon=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}L^k\epsilon$,
where $d$ is the number of nodes and $L$ is strictly triangular in topological
order.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Add and subtract $\hat f_i(v_{\mathrm{Pa}(i)})$ and apply the triangle
inequality. The local approximation bounds one term; the coordinate Lipschitz
inequality bounds the other. Clamps are identical by assumption. Substitution
in topological order gives the path sum. A strictly triangular $d\times d$
matrix satisfies $L^d=0$, so the finite geometric sum is its inverse.
\end{proof}
The bound can amplify a small local error along deep or high-gain paths.
Observed-parent test MSE is neither a uniform $\epsilon_i$ nor a verified
Lipschitz constant. Our residual and support diagnostics are explanatory
measurements, not certified instances of this proposition.

\begin{proposition}[Root interventions need not constrain propagation]
Consider the known graph $R\to M$, $R\to Y$, $M\to Y$, with
$M=R$ and $Y=f(R,M)=R+M$. For any $K\notin\{-1,1\}$, the alternative
mechanism $g_K(R,M)=R+M+K(M-R)$ uses both declared parents and agrees with
$f$ under every intervention on $R$ alone. If a learned upstream mechanism
instead predicts $\hat M=R+\delta$, its free-running prediction using $g_K$
has error $|(1+K)\delta|$, although its observed-parent fit is exact.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Root interventions leave the parent support of $Y$ on the diagonal $M=R$,
where the added term vanishes. Substitution of $\hat M$ gives
$g_K(R,\hat M)=2R+(1+K)\delta$, whereas the true outcome is $2R$.
Both parent coefficients are nonzero for the stated choices of $K$.
\end{proof}
This failure need not leave a coordinate bounding box. For example, take
$R=0.25$, $\delta=0.01$ and $K=1000$. Both true and predicted parent vectors
are inside $[-1,1]^2$, but the prediction error is $10.01$. Observations
covering the diagonal cannot constrain sensitivity normal to it; more root
observations alone do not remove that ambiguity. An internal intervention can
distinguish these mechanisms. Our prospective root-only protocol assesses
prediction under held-out root interventions, not unrestricted internal-action
identification. This is an existence example, not a diagnosis of a measured
failure or a verified bound for the fitted networks.

\begin{proposition}[Quantization margin]
For $m\geq2$ distinct ordered levels $\ell_1<\cdots<\ell_m$, let $Q$ assign the nearest
level, with a fixed tie rule, and let
$b_k=(\ell_k+\ell_{k+1})/2$. Define
$M(y)=\min_k|y-b_k|$. If $|\hat y-y|<M(y)$ then
$Q(\hat y)=Q(y)$. If a pointwise prediction bound $B(y)$ is valid,
$\Pr(Q(\hat y)\ne Q(y))\leq\Pr(M(y)\leq B(y))$ under any specified deployment
probability measure for which the bound holds almost surely.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The open interval of radius $M(y)$ around $y$ crosses no decision boundary.
Consequently both values occupy the same decision cell. The contrapositive
implies that a quantization error requires $M(y)\leq|\hat y-y|\leq B(y)$.
Strict inequality excludes boundary ties.
\end{proof}
Snapped error depends on the location of errors relative to decision boundaries;
similar MSE alone need not imply similar exact-level accuracy.
This finite-level example clips outside values to endpoint cells. The empirical
scorer instead extends the lattice past both endpoints. The same argument uses
the complete set of boundaries of that extended quantizer; at a recorded true
level, its nearest boundary distance remains half the adjacent gap. The
empirical evaluation measure is uniform over the archived grid.

\paragraph{Counterexamples.}
For a constant predictor and test response zero, fitting a single training
response zero gives zero risk; adding a noisy response two makes its
least-squares mean one and test risk one. With a noisy training target one,
optimizing a constant from $0.1$ to one reduces training error but increases
test error. For a two-stage chain, suppose the true parent and child are zero
on the observed support, the learned parent predicts $0.1$, and the learned
child is $\hat f(z)=100z$. The child has zero observed-parent error at zero,
but its free-running prediction is ten. These are existence examples, not
descriptions of the measured histories. Analytically solved chains, forks,
colliders and clamps are checked by executable tests.

\begin{abstract}
A sequential experimental learner need not be the best model delivered after
collection ends. We investigate whether retaining and refitting a completed
experimental history improves the resulting causal surrogate, and which parts
of the delivery procedure account for that change. A completed confirmation on
one fixed deterministic emulator covers \DeliveryN{} acquisition histories:
refitting all eligible paid observations reduces geometric mean snapped
exact-level error to \DeliveryRatio{} times the online learner's error
(95\% interval \DeliveryCILow{}--\DeliveryCIHigh{}), with
\DeliveryWins{} histories improving. This comparison combines data reuse and
additional optimization; it does not identify an architecture effect or an
advantage at equal fitting compute. An exploratory attribution study on the
same exposed grid gives continuous-error ratios of \AFlatRatio{} versus a
development-selected flat refit and \AMatchedRatio{} versus a matched-CPU flat
fit, using fixed initialization zero. We give conditional statements for
mechanism eligibility, deterministic error propagation and quantization
sensitivity, with explicit counterexamples to monotonic improvement.
An archived physical case study retains all eleven conditions: delivery beats
rolling learning in \CRollingWins{} conditions but Fourier regression in
\CFourierWins{}. A frozen independent-system comparison is in progress and its results remain
pending. The present evidence supports a scoped
post-collection delivery finding, without an acquisition or foundation-model
claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
An experimental learner has two jobs: maintain a useful model while observations
arrive, and deliver a predictor after collection ends. These jobs can favor
different uses of the same paid observations. A rolling buffer limits update
cost, but it also forgets earlier parts of the experimental support. A completed
history permits a fresh batch fit, whose extra computation is spent after the
experimental responses have already been obtained. Evaluating only the online
learner can therefore understate the predictive value of the collected data.
Conversely, an improvement after refitting can be wrongly credited to causal
factorization when ordinary data retention or optimization explains it.

We study this distinction for structural causal model (SCM) surrogates with a
known graph and measured intermediate variables. A mechanism network is fit on
observed parents, but a deployed chain must use its own predicted intermediates.
Thus a local regression improvement and a downstream prediction improvement
are different outcomes. Intervention masks determine which observations can
estimate each mechanism, and the joint parent support determines where such
estimates are constrained. These details matter even when every measured
variable lies inside its training range.

Our central question is empirical: \emph{what does a completed experimental
history buy at delivery time, and what accounts for that benefit?} We investigate
three explanations: retaining observations that a rolling buffer discards,
using paid observations that never entered a live update, and performing more
optimization. A direct flat predictor and fixed classical regressors test
whether a simpler refit can explain the result. We use ``delivery'' to denote
this post-collection fitting procedure, without implying a new optimization
algorithm or a general identification guarantee.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
First, we document a completed comparison over repeated histories of one fixed
emulator and an exploratory factorial attribution with unchanged original
weights and explicit fitting costs. Second, we formalize the eligibility of
interventional observations for mechanism regression and explain the distinction
between observed-parent fit and free-running prediction through propagation
bounds and support counterexamples. These elementary results clarify the
empirical estimand and its limits; they are not new general causal-identification
theorems. Third, we report an archived physical prediction study with domain
controls and all conditions retained, including unfavorable results. A frozen
prospective study tests the selected recipe on independent parameterizations
and reserved action blocks; its outcomes remain pending in this working version.

Within the scratch-fit matrix, long fits on retained histories outperform
final-buffer fits and 100-update fits; adding unused paid rows changes aggregate
continuous error much less. These conditional recipe contrasts do not partition
the original online-to-delivery gain. The matrix also favors the structured recipe against the selected flat
controls on the exposed evaluation grid. Differences in usable rows,
intermediate supervision and computation limit that interpretation. The physical
study supplies a complementary boundary: a domain-informed Fourier predictor
outperforms neural delivery in every evaluated condition. Our contribution is
therefore an assessment of delivery behavior and accounting, with its scope
fixed by the controls and evaluation design.

\section{Setting and delivery procedure}
\label{sec:setting}
Let $G$ be a known DAG, $\mathrm{Pa}(i)$ the parents of node $i$, and $R$ its
root nodes. A paid history is a sequence
$\mathcal H=\{(a_t,v_t)\}_{t=1}^{T}$, where $a_t$ specifies the intervention
set $I_t$ and its clamped values, and $v_t$ records all measured nodes. A response
is counted when obtained, including duplicate commands and probes that were
not chosen for an online update. Observation reuse adds fitting computation,
not experimental responses.

For an invariant deterministic mechanism $v_i=f_i(v_{\mathrm{Pa}(i)})$, define
its eligible rows by $E_i(\mathcal H)=\{t:i\notin I_t\}$. With a retained row
set $S$, the delivery head minimizes the empirical objective
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal L}_{i,S}(\theta_i)=
 \frac{1}{|S\cap E_i|}\sum_{t\in S\cap E_i}
 \bigl(\hat f_{i,\theta_i}(v_{t,\mathrm{Pa}(i)})-v_{t,i}\bigr)^2.
 \label{eq:local-objective}
\end{equation}
The objective requires $|S\cap E_i|>0$; an empty eligible set supplies no fitted
mechanism and blocks a delivery claim for that head. This objective uses measured parents during fitting. At deployment, each
non-clamped node is evaluated in topological order at predicted parents;
clamped nodes take their specified values. We call this \emph{free-running}
prediction. For noisy systems, conditional-mean regression is justified only
under the assumptions in Section~\ref{sec:theory}; recursively composing such
means is not generally an interventional-mean estimator.

\paragraph{Recipe and simpler control.}
The selected delivery recipe fits separate two-hidden-layer ReLU networks of
width 64 from scratch, with full-batch Adam, learning rate $0.002$, and 30,000
updates per head. Initialization zero is the deployment specification. A
100-update version is the optimization ablation. The simpler control uses the
same MLP form for a direct root-to-target regression. It predicts the composed
map without supplying intermediate labels. In the archived attribution,
intermediate-intervention rows are excluded from this flat regression because
its root-only inputs cannot represent those responses. In the prospective
root-only design, all training rows are eligible for every nonroot head and
for the flat predictor. Intermediate supervision and total parameter counts
still differ. These comparisons evaluate complete recipes rather than isolating
architecture with identical supervision.

\paragraph{Endpoints.}
For a target $Y$ and an evaluation action set $\mathcal A$, continuous MSE is
$|\mathcal A|^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}(\hat y(a)-y(a))^2$.
Prospective normalized MSE (NMSE) divides this quantity by the target variance
in the corresponding training history, using no held-out responses to set the
scale. Archived attribution instead normalizes by the population target variance
of its exposed grid, shared across arms; physical errors use each condition's
training target variance. Exact-level error is the fraction with
$Q(\hat y)\ne Q(y)$ for the fixed archived quantizer. It extends the recorded
level lattice beyond both endpoints using the adjacent end spacing and resolves
midpoint ties downward, rather than clipping predictions to endpoint levels.
Nonfinite predictions are invalid for scientific acceptance.
It is secondary for genuinely quantized targets outside the archival confirmation;
it is not interchangeable with continuous prediction error. We distinguish
exposed-grid assessment, which includes acquired inputs, from action-block
holdout evaluation.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Causal models and invariance.}
Neural causal models connect expressive function classes to structural causal
inference, but expressiveness does not itself establish learnability or
identification \citep{xia2021causal}. Our setting supplies the graph and measured
parents rather than recovering causal structure from unrestricted observations.
Invariant prediction uses stable relationships across experimental settings
for causal inference \citep{peters2016invariant}. Here invariance is an assumption
supporting eligible-row regression; we do not perform invariant-prediction
structure selection or claim its confidence guarantees. Work on causal
representation learning addresses learning causal variables and mechanisms
from observations \citep{scholkopf2021representation}. We assume the variables
are already measured, so representation discovery is outside the study.

\paragraph{Online learning and final prediction.}
Obtaining a final predictor from sequential learning has a substantial
literature. Online-to-batch results can extract predictors with risk bounds
from online hypotheses under specified sampling assumptions
\citep{cesabianchi2004generalization}. Our procedure instead fits anew on a
retained interventional history and measures the data-use and compute changes.
Its adaptive histories and neural optimization do not inherit those bounds.
Batch refitting and replay are existing tools; the contribution here is the
controlled account of which rows, fitting budgets and deployment semantics
explain the observed delivery behavior.
GDumb already stores streaming examples and trains from scratch at test time
\citep{prabhu2020gdumb}; experience replay retains earlier samples during
continual learning \citep{rolnick2019replay}. Thus separating collection from
scratch fitting is established. Our specific assessment concerns intervention
eligibility, the difference between measured-parent fitting and predicted-parent
deployment, admitted-versus-paid response accounting, and constrained recipe
comparisons, rather than a new temporal fitting principle.

\paragraph{Prediction-dependent inputs and physical validation.}
Sequential prediction can fail when an earlier prediction changes later inputs;
imitation-learning reductions address this distribution mismatch
\citep{ross2011reduction}. A DAG surrogate similarly receives predicted parents
at deployment, but we do not apply that algorithm or transfer its regret
results to our intervention setting. Our deterministic bound states the needed
support and Lipschitz assumptions directly. Causal Chambers supply manipulable
physical systems with published causal models \citep{gamella2025chambers}. We use
one archived light-tunnel task as a prediction check against physical and
Fourier bases, rather than claiming a new apparatus or a live action-design
experiment.

\section{Completed delivery confirmation}
\label{sec:confirmation}
The comparison retains the originally registered \DeliveryN{} histories of a
single deterministic emulator. The delivery procedure fits separate mechanism
MLPs from all eligible paid rows using full-batch Adam and additional epochs.
Original online weights remain unchanged. Evaluation runs the learned chain on
the full previously exposed discrete grid using predicted intermediate values.
Acquired input points occur within this grid, so it is not a held-out prospective
sample.

The primary endpoint is exact-level error after mapping predictions to the
nearest target level. For each history, the registered numerator is the median
of three scored optimization initializations, and the denominator is the original
online chain's error. That median summarizes sensitivity; it is not a deployable
rule for selecting a model without access to true test outcomes. Histories,
not initialization repeats or grid rows, are the paired units.

The geometric mean paired ratio is \DeliveryRatio{}, with a log-scale
$t$ interval of [\DeliveryCILow{}, \DeliveryCIHigh{}] and exhaustive two-sided
sign-flip $p=\DeliveryP{}$. \DeliveryWins{} of \DeliveryN{} histories improve.
The worsening history \WorseSeed{} changes from \WorseOnline{} online error
to \WorseDelivery{} delivery median error (ratio \WorseRatio{}); it is retained
without exclusion or rescue tuning. The registered numerical floor is below every observed paired error and does
not alter these ratios. The interval is conditional on the history sampling
for this fixed emulator; interpreting the sign-flip test requires symmetry
of the paired log differences under its null. The generated values and source
hashes are listed in \texttt{claim\_index.json}.

This supports the bundled delivery procedure on repeated histories of that
emulator. Paid histories are matched, but fitting compute is not. It establishes
neither acquisition superiority, causal-factorization attribution, a foundation
model benefit, nor generalization over independently parameterized systems.

\section{Exploratory attribution of the delivery benefit}

\subsection{Design and controls}
We compare three unique-row data sets: the final 50-entry buffer, all rows
actually admitted at live online updates, and all eligible paid rows. Each is

scheme, optimizer and intervention masks. The original online model is a
separate comparator. Flat MLPs have both equal-epoch and separately labeled
matched-CPU comparisons. Fixed ridge, polynomial and tree controls are
configured on an earlier development history, with no configuration tuning on
the twelve exposed histories.
Input ranges are recomputed from each row set's eligible observations. Retention
contrasts therefore change both rows and training-derived scaling; identical
parameter seeds need not give identical initial functions in physical coordinates.
The all-paid 100/30,000 contrast holds the rows and scaling fixed.

Replay reconstruction reveals additional differences from the online training
path: the selected observation is duplicated in consolidation; per-node fast
adaptation can include a directly intervened node; and the final observation
refresh enters the saved buffer after the last fit. These differences are
recorded rather than silently attributed to data quantity. The scratch factorial
controls masks among its cells but does not exactly replay the online objective.
No selected clamped-node fast updates occurred in these archived confirmation
histories, so that code-path limitation is not an observed explanation of their
delivery improvement.
Diagnostics retain support coverage, observed-parent residuals, propagated
prediction shifts and decision margins, especially for the worsening history.
No simulator responses are acquired in Stage A.

\subsection{Results and interpretation}

\section{Frozen prospective evaluation}
\label{sec:prospective}
\textbf{Results pending.} This section describes the released protocol, frozen
before collecting responses or opening held-out outcomes. It supplies no
prospective performance claim. The recipe, simpler control and short-fit

\subsection{Systems, histories and estimand}
We use 20 independently parameterized systems in each of two graph-size strata.
The five-node family is an explicit parameterized extension of the audited
legacy equations, whose unparameterized base is fixed; varying collection seeds
alone would not make independent systems. The thirty-node family reuses the
audited graph and coefficient generator with both random states pinned.
These are controlled extensions within the specified families, not an external
benchmark or a representative sample of all SCMs. Appendix~\ref{app:systems}
gives the mechanisms and action construction.
The five-node target depends effectively on one root; its other branch is
irrelevant to that target. The families differ in structure and functions as
well as node count. Graph size labels strata and does not isolate a scaling effect.

Each system supplies two shared 400-response histories: balanced varied-value
root interventions and matched random root interventions. Each also has one
shared set of 400 held-out root actions. Complete joint-action blocks are
reserved before collection and cannot appear in either training history.
All arms receive the same paid history. This design tests dependence on the
collection strategy without establishing acquisition superiority. The target
is $X_3$ in the five-node family and $X_{30}$ in the thirty-node family.

Collection and evaluation both disable noise and target the same deterministic
structural map. This estimand does not substitute a composed conditional mean
for the expected outcome of a noisy nonlinear SCM. The first 50 paid training
responses provide a common, fixed input calibration for structured learners
before their retrospective updates. Thus the rolling comparator reproduces
the archived online update kernel after calibration; it is not a streaming
algorithm that operates before those calibration observations are available.
The primary NMSE denominator uses the full training-history target variance.

\subsection{Comparators and separation of fitting from evaluation}
The primary comparison uses initialization zero for delivery, the original
rolling update kernel, the selected flat refit and the 100-update SCM ablation.
Initialization one and two fits of delivery and flat measure optimization
sensitivity separately; no scored initialization is selected for deployment.
There are eight fits per history, totaling 640 fits across the frozen systems.
All model states, optimizer accounting, inputs and attempt receipts must be
sealed before the evaluator generates any held-out response. Failed or
interrupted attempts remain in custody and block acceptance; they are not
replaced by favorable worlds or removed from the analysis.

Joint root interventions leave every nonroot mechanism eligible. Nevertheless,
SCM and flat learners differ in intermediate supervision and total fitting
compute. The comparison therefore tests the selected recipe against a simpler
predictor, not an architecture effect at equal supervision or compute. Root-only
histories can leave parent support on a lower-dimensional manifold, so this
study also does not establish identification for arbitrary internal interventions.

\subsection{Registered primary analysis}
For system $s$, history $h$, and control $c$, define
\begin{equation}
 z_{s,c}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{h\in\{\mathrm{balanced},\mathrm{random}\}}
 \left[\log\max(\mathrm{NMSE}_{s,h,\mathrm{delivery}},\eta)
       -\log\max(\mathrm{NMSE}_{s,h,c},\eta)\right],\qquad\eta=10^{-12}.
 \label{eq:paired-analysis}
\end{equation}
The numerical floor and its activation count are reported. The independent
unit is the system, with histories weighted equally within it. Each stratum's
ratio is $\exp(\bar z_c)$ with a marginal 95\% $t$ interval on the mean log
ratio and a two-sided paired $t$ test across its 20 systems. Holm adjustment
covers four primary tests: delivery versus rolling and delivery versus flat
in each of the two strata. An asserted superiority requires all three frozen
conditions: ratio at most $0.8$, upper marginal interval below one, and
Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$. The intervals themselves are not simultaneous intervals.
These parametric intervals and tests rely on system-level log-ratio sampling
assumptions; they are not distribution-free guarantees. Ablation, initialization
sensitivity, history-specific summaries and failure diagnostics are reported
separately. No grid rows, histories or optimization
repeats are counted as independent systems. No result is accepted from an
incomplete primary matrix.

\label{sec:reproducibility}
Fitting and evaluation run in separate processes. Training inputs, row selections,

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Data retention and fitting effort.}
The exploratory contrasts distinguish keeping a completed history from keeping
only the final buffer, and a short scratch fit from a much longer fit. Both
changes are consequential within the scratch-fit matrix. Retention also changes
normalization, while the online comparator carries persistent weights and Adam
states; this matrix does not isolate their separate causal effects.
The all-paid versus admitted-row aggregate continuous contrast is small, but
has marked history-level and endpoint heterogeneity. This distinction is useful for experimental
systems: recording observations and budgeting a final fit can improve delivery
without a change in acquisition. It does not imply that more data or more
updates improve every system, as the retained failure and counterexamples show.

\paragraph{Structured prediction and support.}
A known graph gives a decomposition and a rule for row eligibility. It also
creates a deployment dependency: upstream error changes the inputs of later
heads. A low observed-parent residual cannot by itself validate the deployed
chain, and a coordinate bounding box cannot establish joint support.
Intermediate-intervention measurements may constrain mechanisms that a
root-only predictor cannot learn from those inputs, but no pure factorization
advantage follows from a comparison with unequal usable supervision. The
prospective protocol deliberately tests prediction of root-interventional maps;
its results will not resolve the internal-action ambiguity.

\paragraph{External boundary and reporting units.}
The physical task is a single mechanism in one apparatus. Neural delivery wins
some comparisons with a rolling learner, but loses every comparison with the
joint Fourier predictor. This limits claims about a generally preferable
neural recipe and gives a concrete example where domain structure is more
useful. Condition-specific bootstrap intervals describe observed action blocks,
not a population of independently built apparatuses. Similarly, the archived
histories supply conditional evidence about one emulator, not twelve causal
worlds. The prospective systems address variation within two generator
families, with broader physical generalization still outside their scope.

\paragraph{Remaining evidence and validity limits.}
The archived evaluation grid was previously exposed and includes acquired
inputs. Attribution on that grid is exploratory, even though its configurations
were fixed before the new fits. The confirmation's median of three scored
initializations describes sensitivity and must not be read as a deployable
model-selection rule. The prospective fixed-initialization protocol is needed
to test the selected recipe on unused action blocks. Compute totals distinguish
fitting process time from full execution and requested allocations; an
equal-epoch neural comparison is not an equal-compute comparison. The eligibility
and propagation propositions require measured parents, invariance, noise and
support assumptions that fitted residuals cannot certify. Causal discovery,
counterfactual identification and foundation-model capability are not tested.

\section{Conclusion}
A completed experimental history can support a substantially different delivered
predictor from the learner maintained during collection. Within the archived
scratch-fit matrix, long fits on retained histories improve substantially over
final-buffer and short fits, while unused paid rows have a smaller aggregate
continuous effect with substantial heterogeneity. This is a conditional recipe
comparison, rather than a partition of the online-to-delivery gain. Structured delivery remains
favorable against the selected simpler refits on the exposed grid, while a
physical prediction study shows a clear boundary against domain-informed
controls. The theoretical analysis explains why eligible-row fitting, joint
support and free-running error must be considered separately. The frozen
prospective comparison remains the outstanding test of independent-system
prediction; until its outcomes are validated, the empirical conclusion is
restricted to the completed archived studies.

$\mathrm{Linear}(d,64)$--ReLU--$\mathrm{Linear}(64,64)$--ReLU--

per fitted head, not an acquired observation.

\subsection{Archived emulator and acquisition histories}
The archived task is a hand-coded deterministic emulator, rather than a physical
world. It has eight controllable roots and five measured nonroot mechanisms:
two link scores $L,M$, their combined score $I$, the target $Y$ and a separate
cost branch $C$. Write the roots as $(N,D,h,p,N_M,h_M,p_M,J)$, representing
total and dynamic counts, altitude and power for the first link, count, altitude
and power for the second, and shared jamming. Their five-level supports are
\begin{align*}
 N&\in\{250,350,450,550,650\},&D&\in\{0,50,100,150,200\},\\
 h&\in\{250,300,350,400,450\},&p&\in\{1,1.25,1.5,1.75,2\},\\
 N_M&\in\{5,10,15,20,25\},&h_M&\in\{6000,7000,8000,9000,10000\},\\
 p_M&\in\{1,1.25,1.5,1.75,2\},&J&\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}.
\end{align*}
Let $s(z)=\min(1,\max(0,z))$ and $d=D/N$. The fixed mechanisms are
\begin{align*}
 L={}&s\left(0.35s\left(\frac{N+0.3D-100}{600}\right)
 +0.25s\left(1-\frac{|h-350|}{300}\right)
 +0.40s\left(\frac{p(1+0.15d)-0.8}{1.2}\right)
 -0.30s(J(1-0.6d))\right),\\
 M={}&s\left(0.25s\left(\frac{N_M-5}{20}\right)
 +0.30s\left(1-\frac{|h_M-8000|}{4000}\right)
 +0.45s\left(\frac{p_M-0.8}{1.2}\right)-0.25s(0.85J)\right),\\
 I={}&s(0.55L+0.45M),\qquad
 Y=\frac{\operatorname{round}_{\rm even}(30s((I-0.35)/0.50))}{30},
 \qquad C=N+0.75D.
\end{align*}
The shared $J$ is a fork and $I$ is a collider; $C$ never feeds the target.
Response generation follows frozen Python rounding; scorer midpoint ties are
separately defined above. The 31 target levels are $\{k/30:k=0,\ldots,30\}$.
Every one of the $5^8=390{,}625$ root actions receives equal weight in the exposed
grid. Its target variance normalizes archived continuous errors.

Each independently seeded history runs 200 iterations with four random proposed
probes per iteration and outcome-dependent mechanism selection, no model API
or policy update. Frozen settings include three initial rows, 500 validation
rows, 100 learner epochs, a 50-entry buffer and a 25-row observation refresh
every five iterations. All paid calls, including unselected probes, are logged.
Case seeds derive from the first 32 SHA-256 bits of a fixed pre-outcome
case-index string modulo $2^{31}-1$; the full seed rule and settings accompany
the frozen protocol. Independence concerns these acquisition streams, not
different mechanisms.

\section{Prospective systems and action construction}
\label{app:systems}
The five-node family has roots $X_1,X_4$ and mechanisms
\begin{align*}
 X_2&=aX_1+b,&
 X_3&=cX_1-dX_2+s\sin(X_2),&
 X_5&=qX_4^2.
\end{align*}
Independent coefficient draws multiply base coefficients
$(a,b,c,d,s,q)=(2,1,0.5,1,1,0.2)$ by independent uniform factors in
$[0.8,1.2]$. The thirty-node family has five layers of sizes $(5,5,10,5,5)$ and edge
coefficients drawn uniformly in $[0.3,0.7]$. Nodes in the second layer choose
one or two roots uniformly without replacement. In the third layer, nodes
whose one-based index is divisible by three choose two second-layer parents;
other nodes choose one. Fourth-layer nodes choose two or three third-layer
parents uniformly without replacement. Each final-layer node chooses one
fourth-layer parent. Graph and coefficient random states are pinned by the
system descriptor; no edge or coefficient is redrawn per response. For a nonroot
$i$, write $u_i=\sum_{j\in\mathrm{Pa}(i)}w_{ij}X_j$; its structural response
is $u_i+0.2\sin(u_i)$ when its one-based node index is divisible by five,
and $u_i$ otherwise. Both families use zero exogenous noise in this study.
All graph and coefficient descriptors precede response generation and are
shared unchanged across arms.

Each root action lies in $[-3,3]$. Joint blocks are Cartesian products of five
width-$1.2$ bins, with the upper endpoint assigned to the last bin. A fixed hash
of each block reserves approximately one fifth of blocks for evaluation.
Random training and evaluation actions are uniform draws accepted only in
their respective block partitions. Balanced training favors the least-used
of eleven evenly spaced levels at each root, subject to the same block
exclusion; the bounded tie and quota rules are frozen in the implementation.
Duplicate commands remain charged observations. No observed outcome determines
which action or world enters the frozen design.

Mechanism input ranges are fixed from the first 50 paid training rows;
root ranges use the declared $[-3,3]$ support. For initialization $r$, each
nonroot head is seeded by $r$ plus its position in the topological order; the
flat head uses $r$ plus the number of nodes. These deterministic offsets are
shared across the corresponding structured arms. No outcome selects an offset,
initialization or replacement world.

\section{Physical split and uncertainty}
\label{app:physical}
The archive task uses the two polarizer commands and the measured light response
\texttt{vis\_3}. Angles are in $[-90,90)$ degrees. For command pair
$(\theta_1,\theta_2)$, define $b_j=\lfloor(\theta_j+90)/30\rfloor$.
A joint block is held out if $(b_1+2b_2)\bmod5=0$; identical commands and their
repeated readings always share a block. The development condition
\texttt{white\_64} is excluded from the eleven-condition evaluation.
Neural output centering and scaling use the training mean and standard
deviation, and are inverted before scoring; neural angle inputs are divided by
90. The neural delivery fit uses
30,000 full-batch updates. Rolling fitting follows archive order, using the
last 50 eligible training rows and 100 updates per arriving row with persistent
Adam. Its retrospective access to the training normalizer is shared with
delivery and is not a strictly streaming normalization claim.

Physics regression fits an intercept and $\cos^2(\theta_1-\theta_2)$ after
conversion to radians. Fourier regression uses the 25 tensor-product features
from $\{1,\sin(2\theta),\cos(2\theta),\sin(4\theta),\cos(4\theta)\}$ at each
angle. Both fit linear coefficients on training rows only. Conditional intervals
use 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples of the eight held-out action blocks within
each condition, with fixed bootstrap seed 620061. Resampling gives each block
equal weight; the primary descriptive NMSE instead weights readings equally.
This difference explains why a block-weighted ratio need not equal a ratio
computed from the row-weighted table. Neither bootstrap establishes uncertainty
across independently constructed physical systems. The intervals are a conditional
resampling assessment under block exchangeability; archive-order dependence or
unobserved action blocks can limit that interpretation.
