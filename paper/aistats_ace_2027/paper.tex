\begin{abstract}
A sequential experimental learner need not be the best model delivered after
collection ends. We investigate whether retaining and refitting a completed
experimental history improves the resulting causal surrogate, and which parts
of the delivery procedure account for that change. A completed confirmation on
one fixed deterministic emulator covers \DeliveryN{} acquisition histories:
refitting all eligible paid observations reduces geometric mean snapped
exact-level error to \DeliveryRatio{} times the online learner's error
(95\% interval \DeliveryCILow{}--\DeliveryCIHigh{}), with
\DeliveryWins{} histories improving. This comparison combines data reuse and
additional optimization; it does not identify an architecture effect or an
advantage at equal fitting compute. An exploratory attribution study on the
same exposed grid gives continuous-error ratios of \AFlatRatio{} versus a
development-selected flat refit and \AMatchedRatio{} versus a matched-CPU flat
fit, using fixed initialization zero. We give conditional statements for
mechanism eligibility, deterministic error propagation and quantization
sensitivity, with explicit counterexamples to monotonic improvement.
An archived physical case study retains all eleven conditions: delivery beats
rolling learning in \CRollingWins{} conditions but Fourier regression in
\CFourierWins{}. Independent-system comparisons remain necessary before
submission. The present evidence supports a scoped
post-collection delivery finding, without an acquisition or foundation-model
claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Experimental collection and final model delivery have different constraints.
An online learner must update quickly and may discard observations to bound
memory or computation. After collection, a separate fit can use retained
observations, including paid probes and former validation rows. Any apparent
benefit then has several possible explanations: a larger retained data set,
more fitting compute, different optimization history, or the use of a known
mechanism graph. Our aim is to separate these explanations.

The completed comparison supplies evidence for a delivery recipe on one
emulator. The experimental program below is designed to test whether that
recipe has a distinct causal-surrogate contribution. An ordinary flat refit
explaining the result would narrow the contribution to delivery and experimental
accounting. We will retain that conclusion rather than infer a more general
architectural advantage from a favorable aggregate. Completed exploratory
attribution supports carrying the structured recipe to a prospective test,
while retaining its worsening history and limitations.

\paragraph{Current contributions and scope.}
We report a completed, receipt-backed comparison across repeated acquisition
histories of one fixed system; derive conditions and limitations relevant to
reuse and chain prediction; and report exploratory attribution that separates
retained data from fitting budget. An archived physical case study establishes
a boundary against strong domain controls; independent-system results are
pending. Neither causal structure discovery nor learned experiment selection is
a current contribution. No foundation model is used in the delivery experiment.

\section{Problem and related work}
We assume a known directed acyclic graph. A surrogate predicts each nonroot
node from its measured parents during fitting and its predicted parents during
free-running deployment. Correct interventions replace the relevant prediction
with its clamped value. A roots-to-target flat predictor supplies a simpler
prediction control; when intermediate mechanisms are intervened on, a flat
predictor without action inputs cannot represent that response using roots alone.
Such rows are excluded from its observational-map fit.

The connection between neural models and SCMs already has substantial theory.
Expressiveness is distinct from causal learnability, and structural constraints
and identification assumptions matter \citep{xia2021causal}. We do not claim
that neural approximation or low MSE identifies an unrestricted SCM. Our
conditional mechanism regression and deterministic propagation statements
address a much narrower known-graph setting. Causal Chambers provide an external
physical apparatus with published causal models \citep{gamella2025chambers};
the archive study assesses physical prediction under that apparatus's
conditions, without claiming new live experiment design.

\section{Completed delivery confirmation}
\label{sec:confirmation}
The comparison retains the originally registered \DeliveryN{} histories of a
single deterministic emulator. The delivery procedure fits separate mechanism
MLPs from all eligible paid rows using full-batch Adam and additional epochs.
Original online weights remain unchanged. Evaluation runs the learned chain on
the full previously exposed discrete grid using predicted intermediate values.
Acquired input points occur within this grid, so it is not a held-out prospective
sample.

The primary endpoint is exact-level error after mapping predictions to the
nearest target level. For each history, the registered numerator is the median
of three scored optimization initializations, and the denominator is the original
online chain's error. That median summarizes sensitivity; it is not a deployable
rule for selecting a model without access to true test outcomes. Histories,
not initialization repeats or grid rows, are the paired units.

The geometric mean paired ratio is \DeliveryRatio{}, with a log-scale
$t$ interval of [\DeliveryCILow{}, \DeliveryCIHigh{}] and exhaustive two-sided
sign-flip $p=\DeliveryP{}$. \DeliveryWins{} of \DeliveryN{} histories improve.
The worsening history \WorseSeed{} changes from \WorseOnline{} online error
to \WorseDelivery{} delivery median error (ratio \WorseRatio{}); it is retained
without exclusion or rescue tuning. The generated values and source hashes are
listed in \texttt{claim\_index.json}.

This supports the bundled delivery procedure on repeated histories of that
emulator. Paid histories are matched, but fitting compute is not. It establishes
neither acquisition superiority, causal-factorization attribution, a foundation
model benefit, nor generalization over independently parameterized systems.

\section{Attribution and prospective evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Stage A: attribution (exploratory; completed).}
We compare three unique-row data sets: the final 50-entry buffer, all rows
actually admitted at live online updates, and all eligible paid rows. Each is
fit from scratch for 100 or 30,000 epochs with fixed architecture, initialization
scheme, optimizer and intervention masks. The original online model is a
separate comparator. Flat MLPs have both equal-epoch and separately labeled
matched-CPU comparisons. Fixed ridge, polynomial and tree controls are
configured on an earlier development history, with no configuration tuning on
the twelve exposed histories.

Replay reconstruction reveals additional differences from the online training
path: the selected observation is duplicated in consolidation; per-node fast
adaptation can include a directly intervened node; and the final observation
refresh enters the saved buffer after the last fit. These differences are
recorded rather than silently attributed to data quantity. The scratch factorial
controls masks among its cells but does not exactly replay the online objective.
No selected clamped-node fast updates occurred in these archived confirmation
histories, so that code-path limitation is not an observed explanation of their
delivery improvement.
Diagnostics retain support coverage, observed-parent residuals, propagated
prediction shifts and decision margins, especially for the worsening history.
No simulator responses are acquired in Stage A.

\paragraph{Attribution findings.}
The full registered matrix completed and passed an independent audit of source,
inputs, eligibility masks, original artifacts, fitted-model hashes and all score
files. With initialization zero, all-paid SCM fitting for 30,000 epochs gives
geometric mean continuous NMSE ratios of \AOnlineRatio{} versus unchanged
online weights, \AFlatRatio{} versus the development-selected flat refit, and
\AMatchedRatio{} versus the separately matched-CPU flat fit. These are
descriptive results from the previously exposed grid, not prospective
superiority tests.

Within the scratch SCM factorial, the all-paid long fit has ratios of
\ABufferRatio{} versus final-buffer long fitting and \AOptimizationRatio{}
versus all-paid 100-epoch fitting. Its ratio versus online-admitted long
fitting is \AUnusedRatio{}: this comparison supplies little evidence of a
benefit from the previously unused paid observations. Thus retaining a completed
history and allocating fitting effort are much larger explanations here than
adding those observations. The registered exploratory rule selects the all-paid
SCM at initialization zero, the frozen flat comparator and the short-fit
optimization ablation for prospective evaluation.

SCM and flat fits share the paid history but differ in which intermediate-clamp
rows can enter their regressions. The structured recipe can reuse measurements
that a roots-only observational predictor cannot represent. Accordingly this
comparison does not isolate a pure architecture effect with identical usable
rows, nor prove general causal identifiability. Matched fit CPU includes every
SCM head, including the communication-cost branch that does not feed the scored
target. The complete matrix consumed \AFitCPU{} fit CPU-core-hours; this excludes
process startup, imports and evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:attribution} retains all
primary configurations; initialization sensitivity is preserved separately.

\paragraph{Retained failure and propagation.}
History \WorseSeed{} still worsens under the primary delivery fit, with snapped
error \AWorseSnapped{}. Its target mechanism's observed-parent MSE is
$\AWorseLocalMSE{}$, while free-running chain MSE is $\AWorseChainMSE{}$;
the propagated prediction-shift MSE is $\AWorseShiftMSE{}$. Only
\AWorseOutsidePercent{}\% of evaluated target parent values lie outside the
training bounding box. This points to an in-box propagation and decision-margin
failure rather than widespread box extrapolation, although a bounding box is
not a support-density certificate and shifted predictions do not add
independently to local residual MSE. The history is retained without tuning a
rescue or selecting a different scored initialization.

\paragraph{Stage B: controlled generalization (gated; pending).}
After attribution, freeze a deployable recipe with initialization zero, the
strongest simpler refit and a decisive ablation. Compare them and the online
learner on independently parameterized five-node and thirty-node systems in
the audited generator families. Both balanced varied-value and matched random
histories are shared across prediction methods. Complete joint-action blocks
are reserved before collection. Continuous held-out normalized MSE is primary;
snapped error is secondary only for genuinely quantized outputs. Histories and
optimization repeats are nested within systems. Paired log-ratio contrasts,
multiple-testing correction, practical thresholds and separate graph-size
reporting are frozen before opening outcomes. The stochastic estimand must be
specified explicitly: composing conditional means through nonlinear downstream
functions does not generally produce their interventional expected outcome.
The primary protocol proposal disables noise during both collection and
evaluation, targeting the same deterministic structural map for every arm.
This setting is labeled as a noise-disabled parameterization of the audited
families; it does not establish a benefit for stochastic interventional means.

\paragraph{Stage C: archived physical prediction (completed).}
We use the eleven previously unscored conditions of \texttt{lt\_malus\_v1};
\texttt{white\_64} remains development only. Duplicate commands and joint action
blocks are grouped before splitting. Compare delivery, rolling-buffer learning,
joint Fourier regression and the published relative-angle physics model, using
continuous prediction error. The physics basis is an intercept and
$\cos^2(\theta_1-\theta_2)$, with degree inputs converted to radians; the earlier
product-form attenuation control is not retained. Conditions and repeated
readings from one apparatus are not independent physical worlds. All conditions
and conditional uncertainty are reported in Tables~\ref{tab:physical} and
\ref{tab:physical-ci}. At fixed initialization zero, delivery has lower
row-weighted NMSE than rolling-buffer fitting in \CRollingWins{} conditions,
than the physics fit in \CPhysicsWins{}, and than Fourier regression in
\CFourierWins{}. These are descriptive counts, not independent-world tests.
The study consumed \CCPU{} fit/evaluation CPU-core-hours, excluding imports
and startup, and acquired no physical responses. All fits were sealed before
held-out losses were computed. Fourier regression outperforming delivery in
every condition rules out an external advantage over that control here; the
fixed domain-informed control provides better predictions. This case contains one
measured response mechanism, so it is not a test of DAG factorization. We retain
the result without changing the recipe after scoring.

\section{Reproducibility and resource accounting}
Fitting and evaluation run in separate processes. Training inputs, row selections,
source, dependency versions, optimizer configuration and outputs are hash-bound.
Stage A fit workers are denied network access and evaluation arrays. The physical
worker uses only registered training rows for fitting and normalization and
seals predictions before computing held-out losses. Exclusive output
paths preserve interrupted attempts and reject replacement. Supervisors enforce
wall-time and memory ceilings. CPU time, wall time, unique eligible rows,
parameters, optimizer updates and memory accompany each fit. Original histories
and weights are retained under local result custody. The claim generator binds
every retained numerical result to its validated receipt. Experimental controls,
analytic checks and the execution ledger are versioned with the manuscript.
No closed-source API is needed for the critical path.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
The current positive confirmation is restricted to a bundled delivery recipe,
a quantized endpoint, and repeated histories of a single fixed emulator on an
exposed evaluation grid. Exploratory continuous-error attribution is favorable,
but physical prediction loses to the Fourier control in every condition.
The evidence does not yet justify a new architecture or equal-
compute superiority. The theoretical statements require graph, invariance,
noise and support assumptions that cannot be inferred from small fitted
residuals. Additional observations and optimization can worsen test performance,
and local accuracy can fail under chain deployment.

Submission requires an attributable benefit, prospective controls, an honest
external boundary, compute accounting and complete reproducibility. If ordinary
refitting or matched compute removes the distinction, a narrower empirical
methodology report is the appropriate outcome. The present draft makes those
unresolved requirements explicit.
