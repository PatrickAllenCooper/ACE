## Required findings

### 1. P1 — Release the startup alarm when transferring deadline ownership to the GPU phase

**Wrapper anchors:** [line 410](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:410), [phase call at line 440](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:440), and [line 451](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:451).

`run_controller` arms an alarm for the original `started + 240` and leaves it active when calling `run_gpu_phase`. The phase records that inherited alarm as `old_due` ([phase line 275](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/llm_intervention_campaign_gpu_phase.py:275)) and treats its expiry as an unconditional work deadline ([line 290](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/llm_intervention_campaign_gpu_phase.py:290)). This condition remains active after first verified GPU work. Consequently, a healthy campaign cannot use the advertised 550-second work window: it is interrupted at roughly 240 seconds even when the first generation met its deadline and every service has fresh progress.

A concrete control-flow example is phase entry at elapsed 100 seconds, first verified work at 130, first service completion at 210, and a progressing second service at 241. Both remaining-service reservation checks can pass, but the inherited alarm kills the second service. The wrapper only moves its alarm to 580 seconds after the entire phase returns, which is too late. The phase's restoration of the old timer also makes a near-boundary successful return vulnerable before that reassignment.

**Required correction:** transfer a work/final deadline, rather than the first-work deadline, at the phase boundary while retaining the original monotonic epoch. The phase already separately enforces first verified work by `outer_started + 240`, per-service startup, freshness, and remaining-service reservations. Do not reset the overall clock or remove those first-work checks.

### 2. P1 — Preserve cleanup ownership through the outer exception path

**Wrapper anchor:** [unconditional timer cancellation at line 539](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:539), reached from the [phase invocation](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:440).

The GPU phase explicitly allows an exception to propagate while owned process-group cleanup remains unconfirmed. In that case it leaves the hard final watchdog armed, with the live process handle captured in its handler ([phase lines 449–464](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/llm_intervention_campaign_gpu_phase.py:449), [restoration decision at line 486](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/llm_intervention_campaign_gpu_phase.py:486)). The pinned helper's [hard handler](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_model_service.py:241) is responsible for a final owned-group `SIGKILL` and bounded exit at the original 580-second boundary.

The wrapper catches that exception, writes `failure.json`, then unconditionally calls `setitimer(..., 0)` and exits. If termination failed or closure could not be confirmed, this removes the remaining cleanup action without acquiring the live handle. Owned descendants may remain until external scheduler cleanup; the wrapper's promised 580-second cleanup boundary is no longer enforced. The phase may also have left TERM/INT ignored while relying on that watchdog.

**Required correction:** agree an explicit failure/cleanup handoff with the GPU-phase author. Either the phase retains control until owned cleanup is confirmed or its hard deadline fires, or the wrapper receives a live ownership/cleanup mechanism and completes that bounded responsibility before exiting. Merely omitting the timer cancellation is insufficient if the supervisor immediately exits, because its alarm then cannot run. Never reconstruct cleanup authority from a saved PID, and do not cancel the watchdog based only on failure-file publication.

### 3. P2 — Validate the pinned output destination before creating it

**Wrapper anchors:** [lines 511–513](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:511), [line 532](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:532), and the later [fixed-output check at line 337](/Users/pat/code/ACE/scripts/research/qualify_llm_intervention_campaign_gpu.py:337).

The initial entry claims the caller-supplied `--output` before reading the pinned packet or checking that it equals `paths.gpu_output`. It also writes `packet.json` there before the fixed-output check. A mistaken `--output <cpu_output>/worker/artifacts/campaign/policy-05` therefore creates a GPU-attempt directory inside the accepted CPU campaign. Admission subsequently rejects the already-started directory, and the exception path adds `failure.json` there. This alters accepted input custody and makes a correctly addressed later invocation fail its campaign inventory/once-only checks, despite no GPU phase having run. The same ordering can place diagnostic files in another unintended new directory under the original CPU inputs/output.

**Required correction:** read the independently pinned packet and validate the supplied output against its fixed, separated destination before any mkdir or failure/packet write. Track ownership only after that validated destination has been claimed. An invalid CLI destination must not mutate the accepted CPU packet, campaign, or inventories.
