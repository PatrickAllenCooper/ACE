# deployment-forensics
## SUMMARY
Per-command scores were NOT saved for any LLM arm at deployment, so score-level state sensitivity (brief item 2) cannot be measured from these records. Scores are saved for the ranker, target risk (TR) and local
  information. I rebuilt the exact public state of every LLM decision by decoding the saved prompt token ids (1,698 decodes, all SHA256-verified), giving one table of all 4,371 decisions of the eight arms. At the shared
  first decision set_x_neg is the TR optimum in 11/24 systems and the best constant action, so DPO starts right and then never adapts: on its own states set_x_neg is optimal in 39/576 decisions and never after decision
  5. The supervised adapter IS state-dependent in its commands (budget level, graph, stopping); the DPO stage erased that. The numerical ranker is almost equally state-blind. Decision-level regret under a 256-draw TR
  score orders the arms like the headline endpoint (Spearman 0.93) with far more resolution. Training records stored beside the deployment data show flawed supervision and a DPO stage that sharpened the SFT prior without
  learning preferences.

## SURPRISES / CONTRADICTIONS
- The brief's file counts are low: 35,582 files under launch/cpu and 11,564 under launch/gpu, not about 7,100 and 2,100. The LLM arms' episode directories are not in the local copies at all.
- The supervised adapter is not state-blind. It keys on budget level, graph and remaining budget, and those patterns mirror training snapshot levels and label statistics rather than the planner.
- The exposed DPO pairs did not favour x- (chosen 14, rejected 9 of 119; x+ had the largest net count). The ranker trained on the same exposure went to x+, the LLM to x-.
- DPO and SFT beat uniform random over the first four decisions; the damage comes from never switching.
- The reference planner's own choices are mostly Monte Carlo ties after the first three decisions, so its argmax is a poor label and a poor agreement target.
- Arms that pick the same first action still see different states from decision 1, so only decision 0 is a matched comparison.

## IMPLICATIONS FOR AGENDA
- Run a score diagnostic before any retraining: score all 9 commands for frozen, SFT and DPO at the 1,698 saved deployment prompts (token ids are saved verbatim) and at controlled edits of the 24 shared first-decision
  states (sign of X mean, cost menu, budget, graph). My rough estimate is about one 20 GB slice-hour. Persist per-command log-likelihoods and branch probabilities in every future deployment record.
- Add decision-level regret under a high-draw target-risk score as a declared endpoint. It separates DPO from random in 20/24 worlds where cost-to-target tied 13/24. Declare its circularity for the TR arm and consider an
  oracle one-step value as the non-circular version.
- Repair supervision first: remove terminal-state stop examples, replace mixed-teacher hard labels with planner score vectors from a 256-draw or better planner, sample states on-policy and at every budget level, and use
  far more than 128 labelled states. The ranker's failure shows the data, not only the language interface, is limiting.
- Do not rerun the DPO recipe (beta 0.1, one pair per record). Require held-out pair accuracy and command entropy as monitored quantities, use all 687 strict pairs with margins, or drop the stage.
- Decode by whole-command likelihood instead of greedy token-tree choice.
- Test a compact, feature-forward state serialization. One readable number, the sign of the X posterior mean, gives 17/24 optimal first decisions (relative regret 0.055 vs 0.256 for constant x-).
- Keep constant-action policies, the one-feature sign rule and a high-draw planner as controls. DPO is exactly 'always x-'.
- Increase benchmark headroom and replicate measurement streams per system; single acquisitions are too noisy for a first-crossing endpoint.

## OPEN QUESTIONS
- Do frozen, SFT and DPO score vectors vary with the state below the argmax, and how large are the command gaps? Only a scoring pass can answer.
- Why does the SFT adapter give x+ about 0.05 probability on training states when x+ is 29 of 160 labels, against 0.41 for x- with 32?
- Why does the frozen model choose observe: first menu item, token prior, or the pooled-branch decoding?
- Is the SFT stop-time correlation with posterior risk (Spearman +0.54, n=23) real uncertainty reading or a cost-structure confound?
- Which teacher produced each supervised label, given only 43% match the planner?
- Would an oracle one-step value computed from the saved true mechanisms rank the arms the same way as the model-based score?

### ADDITIONAL FINDINGS (verifier)
- Endpoint noise floor: SFT and DPO issue identical actions for their first 10-22 acquisitions in every world but have separate measurement streams. Their capped cost differs in 11/24 worlds, and in 8 of those the
  difference was decided inside the identical prefix (for example world 21: 32 vs 12; world 16: 10 vs 22). Those noise-only differences total 54 cost units against a reported mean difference of 0.54. Ten worlds already
  meet the target at initialization, so 14 carry endpoint information.
- Missing LLM scores are recoverable without new environment responses: local sft.adapter.pt and final.adapter.pt hash to the deployed adapter pins (583b1659..., 6f540dfe...), and all 1,698 exact prompt token sequences
  are saved. A Qwen3-0.6B snapshot is in the local cache, pin not verified. I did not run it.
- DPO-stage logs do not explain 'x- only': shifts favour observe in 19/19 exposed pairs containing it (mean +6.7 nats, although observe was the rejected command in 13) and x- in 20/23 (+3.3). The single direct observe-
  vs-x- pair moved 1.37 nats toward observe. Deployed DPO emitted observe 0/576 times. Token-tree pooling, off-distribution states or later updates could explain this; the records cannot separate them.
- Stop appears in 0 of 3,584 preference pairs, yet DPO removed every stop (SFT stopped in 23/24 worlds, DPO in 0/24). The DPO stage changed behaviour that no label addressed.
- Position, identity and tie rule are confounded: menu order is identical in all 4,371 deployment states and 160 training records, and the task payload is byte-identical everywhere. Frozen emits the first menu entry; SFT
  and DPO emit the first listed intervention, which also wins every alphabetical tie rule. Protocol source (llm_intervention_screen_protocol.py:126-132,188) assigns a quarter of labels each to random, menu-coverage,
  local-information and target-risk teachers; read in source, not recomputed per record.
- Off-distribution deployment states: the longest run of one repeated acquisition in any training history is 10 (129/160 have runs of 3 or less). DPO states from decision 11 onward (312/576) contain 11-23 consecutive x-
  rows.
- The ranker is not equally state-blind early: at decision 0 it deviates to x+ in three worlds and is right in all three; relative regret in decisions 0-3 is 0.219 against 0.337 (DPO) and 0.340 (SFT). It then switches to
  x+ on a step-indexed schedule (never x- after decision 11) while the reference prefers x- in 260/288 of those states.
- On regret DPO is indistinguishable from frozen (worse in 12/24 worlds, p 0.79) and worse than SFT in 19-22/24 (p <= 0.001). An all-observe policy has regret 0.654 yet the fourth-best final NMSE, so the one-step proxy
  should be validated before it becomes a primary endpoint.
