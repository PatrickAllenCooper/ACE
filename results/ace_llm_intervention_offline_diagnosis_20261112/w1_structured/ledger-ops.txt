# ledger-ops
## SUMMARY
The cycle ran from the Nov 2 19:33 UTC pivot to the Nov 10 08:51 UTC report: 181 calendar hours, about 39 active hours in six sessions. It made 50 submissions: 49 Slurm jobs (28 completed, 21 failed) plus one rejected by
  Slurm. Allocation was 40,265 CPU-s and 4,458 GPU-slice-s against a self-imposed 57,600 / 7,200 ceiling. 13,735 CPU-s and 2,742 GPU-slice-s remain, which cannot fund a retrain plus a fresh 24-system screen.

Operations dominated. All 49 jobs together ran 4.6 hours and the scientific kernel was about 2.4 hours. By my classification, half of the failed submissions were the custody harness rejecting a valid environment, and
  fixed size and time bounds broke repeatedly as the evidence tree grew to 107,284 files. All four job failures after the scientific freeze were operational.

The first diagnostic in the handoff is nearly free: per-step training records, 1,184 legal-command scores, the adapters and all 1,698 deployment prompts with completions are already on the Mac. No deadline or target
  venue is recorded for the broader paper.

- Local disk state has changed since the ledger: it recorded 12.66 GB available on Nov 10, while a read-only df on Nov 12 shows about 197 GiB available and /Volumes/Aux mounted.
- The contract's 1,200 s finalizer cap was raised to 4,200 s under routine recovery before launch. The job took 1,596 s, so the frozen value would have failed. Routine recovery therefore included a 3.5x wall-time
  increase.
- Margins on successes were thin: CPU campaign 5,728 s of 6,000 s; batch memory within about 3 MiB of the 7,680 MiB request in three stages; finalizer launch readiness 1,174.8 s of a 1,200 s bound.
- Outside my task but verified in reporting01/data01/actions.csv: the learned numerical ranker also collapsed to two actions (set_x_pos 435, set_x_neg 141 of 576). The handoff's behavioural summary names only the LLM
  arms.
- Also outside my task: DPO per-step loss stayed near ln 2 (0.705 mean in the first quarter of steps, 0.679 in the last), while SFT loss fell from 0.482 to 0.214. 2,897 of 3,584 collected preference pairs were ties and

## IMPLICATIONS FOR AGENDA
- Put the saved-artifact diagnosis first. It needs no CURC, no locks and no envelope, and takes about half a day to a day. It still needs a written protocol, new exclusive output locations and no tuning on the 24
  development outcomes.
- Any retrain plus fresh screen exceeds the remaining envelope on CPU by about a factor of two. The proposal should ask Patrick for an explicit new ceiling: roughly 8-15 core-h and 0.8-1.3 slice-h for 24-48 systems, and
  3-5 calendar days.
- A 100-150 system confirmation needs roughly 22-35 core-h and 1.9-2.9 slice-h plus 6-10% for failures, and 6-10 calendar days. It also needs a custody and sharding redesign first, because the 1,770 s archive bound, the
  finalizer's three full-tree verification passes and the 1 h GPU wall will not hold.
- Cost new experiments by the number of Slurm stages and the amount of new harness code, not by compute. Each stage took 0.8-7.7 h of wall-clock and four of seven scientific stage types failed on first submission. Prefer
  designs that reuse accepted packets unchanged.
- Keep any revised training recipe under about 2,500 GPU-s per job, or budget a separate qualification of resumed optimization or a full A100 under gpu-normal. Ten times the current exposure would not fit one gpu-testing
  job with the current checkpoint checks.
- Numerical arms are the largest CPU cost (11,456 of 21,480 campaign CPU-s) because each decision spawns a process. Pruning redundant numerical arms saves proportionally. Moving them in-process would save much more but
  is new harness code needing requalification.
- Do not plan a compute-amortization claim. In-process target-risk latency was never measured, and the observed ordering (LLM episode 17-29 s, numerical episode 28-82 s) is an implementation artefact.
- Front-load the items only Patrick can supply: the envelope decision, a venue or date, Duo authentication once per session, and whether continued scientific use of gpu-testing is acceptable.
