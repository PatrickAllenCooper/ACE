# numerics-protocol
## SUMMARY
The screen used three-node linear-Gaussian SCMs (chain, fork, collider) with publicly known noise and a conjugate per-mechanism Bayesian linear learner. "Matched" means true edges are drawn from the learner's own N(0,1)
  prior; "shifted" means edge mean 1.5 under that same prior; low/high is noise variance 0.25/1.0. The 0.04 threshold and 8+24 budget were prospective diagnostic choices with no attainment or power calculation.

Per head, posterior covariance depends only on design rows. But child rows contain random parent responses unless the parent is clamped, and the target is a product of coefficients across heads, so its variance depends
  on posterior means and the best design is state-dependent. Measured on the 24 saved post-initialization states (working-model expectations, validated by exact replay of saved runs): sequential adaptation after
  initialization is worth about nothing (−0.7% ± 1.7%); a design planned once from the post-initialization state equals target risk; a good schedule depending only on graph and cost menu is 9 to 21% worse in expected
  final NMSE.

Target risk is a noisy 32-draw one-step ratio heuristic, not the Bayes rule for the declared endpoint, yet it sits at the state-conditional optimum of this class. The declared endpoint could not resolve any of this: 10
  systems tie at the floor (9.9 expected from the learner's own posterior), paired SE is 1.1 to 1.4 cost units, and the expected z for state-adaptive versus good static was 0.75 (about 340 systems needed). The eight-arm
  ordering is reproduced from the arms' action sequences alone.

## SURPRISES / CONTRADICTIONS
- The learner's own expected NMSE met the target at initialization in only 2 of 24 systems, yet 10 crossed, and one of those 2 did not cross. Initial crossings are mostly lucky realizations and tiny-effect systems.
- On realized endpoints other than first crossing, DPO is indistinguishable from target risk (sustained crossing +1.33 +/- 2.18; log final NMSE +0.25 +/- 0.39) and coverage has the best geometric-mean final NMSE. 'DPO
  worst' is solid only on design quality (2.1x target risk, better in 0 of 24).
- set_x_neg is the best state-blind first action (best in 11 of 24, median 99% of the best one-step gain) because X=-1 has double query weight. DPO's constant choice is not arbitrary; its failure is never observing and
  never switching side.
- The learned numerical ranker also collapsed: only x- and x+, with an identical sequence of seven x- then seventeen x+ in 10 systems.
- Heterogeneous costs created no budget structure: target risk used cost-2 actions in 16 of 272 decisions.
- Target risk is described as strong, but most late decisions are Monte Carlo ties and local information is within 2 to 6% of it. It looks strong because the class is easy.
- Document drift: the agenda proposes 16+64 responses and seven policies, and the world guide cites 64 actions; the executed screen is 8+24, eight policies, and the effective menu limit is 32.

## IMPLICATIONS FOR AGENDA
- Do not rerun this world class hoping to show an LLM beating target risk. Target risk is at the state-conditional optimum, sequential gain is about zero, and maximum headroom on the endpoint is 3 cost units against a
  paired SE near 1.3.
- Replace the primary endpoint. Use public posterior-risk curves and noise-free design-quality scoring, add replicate episodes and common exogenous noise across arms. Realized first crossing needs hundreds of systems for
  effects that posterior risk shows with under 20.
- Before any LLM training in a new world class, run a GPU-free headroom audit: tuned public-static schedule, plan-once, myopic target risk, and a rollout or dynamic-programming bound. Proceed only where the gap is
  detectable at the planned sample size.
- Diagnose existing and candidate policies with zero new systems by scoring first decisions and action multisets on the saved states. A state-sensitive policy must switch between x- and x+ and must observe.
- Add the controls the screen lacked: a tuned public-only static schedule, plan-once, target risk with a posterior-risk stop rule, and an informed-prior numerical control. This exceeds the current eight-arm limit.
- Candidate successor classes in rising code cost: varied text priors with few initial observations and varied query laws (small to medium); wrong or unknown heteroscedastic noise (small to large); nonlinear truth
  (medium); graph uncertainty (large, the natural home of adaptivity); typed continuous actions (large).
- Repair the task law: remove duplicate queries, vary queries per system, use per-system scale (already supported), and set thresholds by preposterior calculation so initial crossing probability is near zero.
- For confirmation, lift the development-only scope checks in three modules and the 64-world cap, and add an inferential reporting layer; current reporting is descriptive only.

## OPEN QUESTIONS
- What is the exact Bayes-optimal sequential ceiling in this class and in each candidate successor? Not computed here because it requires adaptive rollouts on fantasy systems.
- Do the LLM arms' posterior-risk curves (receipts are only in the CURC archive) agree with the design-quality predictions made from their action sequences?
- How much does knowing the shifted regime help design when initialization is shorter than eight observations? After eight, first-decision margins are already decisive.
- Realized final posterior risk is 3 to 8% below working-model expectation even for static arms in the 12 matched systems. Shared-system sampling variability is my reading; recheck on fresh systems.
- Is the collapse of both learned policies onto x- (the best state-blind first action) traceable to label frequencies in the 160 supervised records?
