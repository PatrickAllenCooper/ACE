# training-forensics
## SUMMARY
The constant action is already in the saved training evidence, so it is not a training-to-development shift. On the 128 nonterminal training prompts the supervised checkpoint generates set_x_neg 108 times and the DPO
  checkpoint 123 times. They match the teacher label in 36 and 32 cases, against 32 for a constant.

Supervised labels are only mildly imbalanced (set_x_neg is 25% of nonterminal targets), but set_x_neg is the plurality at every token decision of the command, and greedy trie decoding follows that path. The 119 DPO pairs
  are not dominated by set_x_neg, and preference labels are not state-independent. They are very noisy: roughly one strict label in three points against the target-risk planner.

The DPO pass moved state-independent command biases rather than state-conditional preferences. A model with no state input reproduces the saved DPO losses.

## SURPRISES / CONTRADICTIONS
- The collapse was visible before deployment in saved training generations and passed acceptance, because the gate checked legality only.
- The supervised model's online loss on new nonterminal states never beat a state-blind label-frequency counter.
- DPO lowered the likelihood of most preferred commands as well as rejected ones. set_x_pos had the largest label net (+9) and lost 3.8 nats.
- The executed tie rule declares about 13-14% of comparisons strict under no difference, despite the agenda's intent to avoid turning noisy differences into preferences (ace_llm_intervention_agenda_2026-11-02.md:91).
- Labels are not pure noise: strict labels agree with the target-risk planner 68.7% of the time, mostly at the initial state. The supervision weakly rewards state dependence, not a constant.
- The final policy is not input-blind: log-odds of _pos vs _neg vary with SD 3.4 nats across states, but the mean bias of -6.9 nats swamps it in 123/128 states.
- The task brief's file counts are off: the local collection holds 30,596 files and the training directory 4,926, not about 12,400 and 235.

## IMPLICATIONS FOR AGENDA
- Do not present the completed DPO arm as a test of preference learning for intervention choice. It tests one label, exposure and optimizer recipe that never learned a state-conditional preference.
- Report from saved files, at no compute cost: training-state action table, command-bias decomposition, label-noise accounting.
- Add a pre-deployment behavioral gate: action distribution and teacher agreement on training and held-out training worlds, plus the state x command share of the score table.
- Fix the preference label before any retraining. Compare expected (planner or exact) gains, many more replicates, and excluding states that already meet the target. A direct check is exact expected gains on the 128
  saved states to measure the true flip rate; this needs authorization because it draws new simulated responses.
- Supervise from one strong teacher with soft labels. The four-teacher mixture includes 25% random labels and a counting rule that emits set_x_neg at every initial state.
- Change the action interface: one distinct token per action, randomized menu order, choice by normalized full-command score.
- Run two cheap controls on the unchanged recipe: randomly flipped pair directions, and labels reassigned to shuffled states. About five minutes of training each on one 20 GB slice. Also test balanced minibatches,
  stronger anchoring and all strict pairs.
- Run the forward-only diagnostic: base, SFT and final command scores on the 160 training prompts and on designed artificial states. The base table is the missing evidence.
- Analyze the numerical ranker's saved training outputs the same way. It used the same exposure, so it separates a supervision failure from a language-interface failure.
- Carry the training bill in any efficiency claim: 9,216 cost units bought 119 used pairs.

### ADDITIONAL FINDINGS (verifier)
- The in-sample 56.8% of a constant ranking is what noise alone gives. Relabelling the 8 actions at random within each record yields 56.5% on average (p 0.45). The coin-flip null (54.1%) ignores within-record dependence.
  There is no detectable state-independent preference signal.
- The label signal that exists is state-specific. Strict labels agree with the state's own target-risk ordering in 68.7% of pairs, but in 50.2% when paired with another state's planner scores (p 0.0005) and 51.4% with
  the average planner ordering. Local information: 67.0% against 51.6% (p 0.0055). This is the positive evidence verdict iii lacked.
- Neither checkpoint tracks labels on its own training states. Final table: teacher label scores -0.16 nats relative to that command's same-snapshot mean (p 0.86). SFT: +0.09 nats (n 27, p 0.18). Within-state, label log-
  likelihood is -2.168 before the model's own SFT update and -2.177 at the end of the pass (n 14).
- The supervised neglect of set_x_pos appeared late. Geometric-mean probability on set_x_pos labels was 0.341 in steps 80-119 and 0.173 in steps 120-159. In the same states it fell from 0.195 to 0.084 by the end of the
  pass (n 6). The last 30 steps hold 8 set_x_neg labels and 2 set_x_pos. Recency weighting gives at most 1.6 to 1, so it is not sufficient. Pass order is an untested contributor that needs a reshuffled rerun.
- Token-greedy decoding is not command argmax. At the SFT checkpoint, snapshots 1-3, saved geometric-mean probabilities are observe_all 0.367 and set_x_neg 0.345, yet greedy output is set_x_neg in 76 of 96 states. In 11
  saved states with P(observe_all) between 0.33 and 0.44 the output is set_x_neg every time. This is an inference from averages.
- One toy counterfactual in the earlier notes did not reproduce. With all 687 strict pairs in random order my re-implementation ends at set_x_pos about 65% of the time, not set_x_neg in 478/500. Which command a state-
  blind learner collapses to is not robust.
- DPO barely fit its own pairs: loss 0.6811 against 0.6931 at the reference, 45/119 moved against their label. It amplified the SFT ordering instead (slope 3.45 of final on SFT score differences, r 0.79).
- Between 69% and 73% of the 687 strict labels are what the tie rule passes under no difference. Mean NMSE reduction from one extra response falls from 0.0230 at snapshot 0 to 0.0013 at snapshot 3, where only 50.0% of
  mean gains are positive.
- Where a teacher's label appears in a strict pair it is preferred 45 times and rejected 11 for local information, against 35/19 for target risk, 30/26 for menu coverage and 16/13 for uniform random.
- The summary sentence that the behavior is not a training-to-development shift holds for the constant action only. SFT never generates stop on nonterminal training prompts (0/128), and stop labels exist only at zero
  budget. The supervised arm's early stopping in deployment therefore sits in budget states training never covered (inference, deployment data not opened). Training histories also come only from the four numerical
  behavior policies.
