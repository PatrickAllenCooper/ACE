## SURPRISES / CONTRADICTIONS
- DPO pairs were not dominated by set_x_neg (chosen 14, rejected 9). set_x_pos was most chosen (23 versus 14) yet lost 3.8 nats. Only a token-level tally (set, _x, _neg net +7, +4, +6) favors the collapsed action. That
  mechanism is my inference.
- The exposure-matched numerical ranker also degenerated (near-uniform scores, two deployed actions), so the failure is not LLM-specific.
- The constant action was already present in generation files written by the training job on 2026-11-08. The acceptance review recorded stop and action counts but no histogram.
- DPO lowered both chosen and rejected likelihoods and erased stop (terminal-state log-probability -3.58), a likelihood-displacement pattern.
- Evidence against total state blindness: the SFT adapter shifts toward observe_all at later snapshots and learned terminal stop, so it reads coarse budget or history-length features.
- Plan flag one_pass_dpo is false only because 568 strict pairs were unexposed, not because pairs repeated. No document disagrees on executed recipe numbers. The agenda's 16+64 budget and seven policies are superseded.

## IMPLICATIONS FOR AGENDA
- Lead with the zero-GPU diagnosis. Saved artifacts already show the constant action at training time, no learning beyond a state-blind baseline, and a DPO stage that failed its own pairs. No new training run is needed
  to establish this.
- Require a behavioral gate in every future training freeze: action histogram, accuracy versus the modal-label baseline, held-out loss versus a state-blind baseline, and held-out pair accuracy versus 50 percent, all
  before deployment.
- Do not scale measured-preference DPO as is. It consumed 9,216 of 10,240 collection cost units for 119 noisy single-pair updates. Prefer listwise soft targets from target-risk scores, or first measure label noise with
  exact expected gains or 16 replicates.
- Repair the supervised target before judging the LLM: one competent teacher or explicit teacher identity, no uniform-random labels, no terminal stop examples, no alphabetical tie-breaks, several shuffled epochs, held-
  out worlds.
- Train the numerical ranker to convergence on CPU as the first learnability probe and as an honest learned control. The current ranker arm is nearly untrained and should not be called a strong control.
- Use free data: 697 saved decision envelopes plus zero-response target-risk relabeling give about five times more states with full score vectors. They share the same 32 worlds, so cross-validate by world.
- Add an action-renaming experiment (single-token labels, permuted order) to test whether the collapsed action follows the label marginal and tie-break order rather than X = -1 itself.
- Engine supports up to 16,384 scheduled steps with repeats, and the whole 279-step run took under five minutes of one 20 GB slice. The exposure planner and scientific worker contract hard-code one pass and need a new
  reviewed version.

- What fraction of the 687 strict preference labels is noise? Exact expected gains from the private world descriptors, or 16 replicates, would settle it.
- Can any learner recover the target-risk action from the saved public state with 128 to about 825 examples? A converged numerical ranker with world-level cross-validation answers this on CPU.
