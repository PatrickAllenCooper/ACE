# DESIGN: evaluation-statistics
## THESIS
Positive form: on fresh systems from a world class that was qualified beforehand by numerical-only headroom criteria, a language-model policy trained to read an explicit SCM state reaches a Bayes-reliable answer at lower expected
  experimental cost than target-risk planning (TR). The effect is replicated across five training seeds, it disappears when the policy's measurements are shuffled or its state is permuted, and it exceeds a numerical student given
  the same information. Every claim is measured with Rao-Blackwellized endpoints whose validity conditions are proved, with common random numbers and a registered TOST null branch. Null or negative form: with measurement repaired
  (CRN, evaluator-side Rao-Blackwellization, a relative cost-to-target band, fresh namespaces) and the study powered for equivalence at a pre-declared margin of 0.3 per-system SD units (about 3 to 9% of expected error), a trained
  small LLM policy is equivalent to or worse than TR. The headroom bounds (T4), the open-loop bound (T5) and the treatment-fidelity diagnostics explain why. In both cases the paper also contributes an evaluation methodology for
  experimental-design policies that needs roughly 8 to 96 times fewer systems than realized-error endpoints. The saved data show this: the implied per-system SD of the log ratio-of-means is 0.16 to 0.38 on posterior error against
  1.13 to 1.57 on realized error. The methodology also shows that the November 24-system screen could not have resolved a realistic benefit: the detectable difference was 3.7 units against a ceiling of 3.04.

### E1 [must] Endpoint non-favorability and planning audit on saved development data
  QUESTION: On the saved 24-system data, do the repaired endpoints resolve policies better than the declared first-crossing endpoint? Does the Rao-Blackwell identity hold? Would adopting the new endpoints favor the LLM arms?
  DESIGN: Offline. No new environment responses, no model execution. Inputs: replayed states of the 5 CPU arms, the decoded public states of all 1,698 LLM decisions (deployment-forensics out/decisions.pkl) and the 192 cost
    reports. For all 8 arms x 24 systems compute: (a) the declared endpoint; (b) learner posterior-risk paths; (c) the evaluator's Rao-Blackwellized error (ERB) under the true generating prior, N(0,1) in matched regimes and
    N(1.5,1) on edges in shifted ones; (d) the relative band cost-to-target, kappa in [1/8,1/2] and [1/4,1/2] of the post-initialization ERB; (e) normalized Bayes risk at the budget; (f) realized-truth versions; (g) TR-256 regret,
    already computed. Final post-acquisition LLM states come from archived receipts if retrieved, otherwise the last decoded state, flagged. Outputs: arm rankings per endpoint, paired SDs and standardized effects for all 28 pairs,
    and the realized/ERB ratio of means by regime family. Sample size: fixed at 24 systems x 8 arms, post hoc, used for planning only. Planning basis already computed from the saved CPU table: paired SD of mean log posterior risk
    0.10 to 0.28 (d 0.43 to 1.74) against realized log NMSE 1.48 to 1.70 (d -0.10 to 0.25).
  DECISION_RULE: An endpoint can be the registered primary only if all three hold. (i) Its median |t| over the 7 contrasts against TR is at least twice the declared endpoint's. (ii) No dev LLM arm improves its position relative to
    TR or uniform random compared with the declared endpoint (non-favorability). (iii) The ratio of mean realized NMSE to mean ERB has a 95% interval containing 1 within both the matched and shifted families; otherwise the ERB
    code is fixed before E2. Positive: adopt the endpoint family, and the paper documents the change with this audit as proof that it is not a favorable replacement. Null (no resolution gain): keep the declared endpoint family,
    but CRN, replicates and at least 300 systems become mandatory. Negative (a candidate endpoint favors the dev LLM arms): it cannot be primary, and this is reported.
  PREREQUISITES: None (saved artifacts on the Mac). Optional: retrieve the final-state receipts of the LLM arms from the CURC archive.
  COST: Under 1 CPU core-hour locally and 1 to 2 analyst days. ERB is closed form using the existing structural_moments recursion. No approval needed.
  MAIN_RISK: The final post-acquisition LLM states are not fully recoverable locally. Planning SDs from 24 systems are uncertain by about +/-30%.

### E2 [must] Evaluation-harness repair and validation on calibration systems (numerical arms only)
  QUESTION: Before any new policy is scored, do common random numbers keyed by action occurrence, evaluator-side Rao-Blackwellization, the relative band endpoint, censoring at the budget, the public stop rule, paired-branch
    attribution and blinded analysis give unbiased, correctly sized, lower-variance contrasts?
  DESIGN: Calibration namespace (new keyed scope): 48 systems from the dev generator (4 regimes x 3 graphs x 2 menus x 2), with per-system query-law and menu-order randomization. CRN: noise U[system, replicate, node, action,
    occurrence] is shared across arms. Arms: TR-32; TR+ (256 draws, CRN across candidate actions); local information; coverage; random; plan-once; an A/A twin (TR-32 with a second planning seed); a planted-effect arm (TR forced to
    spend 25% of budget on observe_all). Replicates: R=8 for the cheap arms (ERB check); R=4 for TR-32, LI, the A/A twin and the planted arm; R=2 for TR+ and plan-once. Independent-noise copies of TR-32, LI and coverage measure
    the CRN variance-reduction factor. Paired-branch attribution (T3) is run for LI vs TR and coverage vs TR on 24 systems with 16 branches. Checks with pre-declared tolerances: (a) CRN leaves marginals unchanged (|delta mean| < 2
    SE, KS p > 0.01). (b) The A/A interval contains 0, and the test's size on 2,000 sign-randomized resamples lies in [0.035, 0.065]. (c) Planted-effect power is within +/-10 points of the formula. (d) The realized/ERB ratio of
    means lies in [0.92, 1.08] with a 95% interval covering 1 in each regime family. (e) Paired-branch attribution agrees with the direct contrast within 2 SE. (f) In matched regimes, mean realized NMSE at the public stop is at
    most kappa*rho0. (g) Variance components sigma_b, sigma_w and rho_CRN are estimated per endpoint. (h) kappa_lo is calibrated so that TR_primary attains it in at least 75% of systems. The same data choose TR_primary (TR-32 or
    TR+) on numerical evidence only. Sample size basis: per-episode CV of about 1.1 over about 1,150 episodes per regime family gives an SE of about 0.032, enough to detect about 9% bias in ERB. 48 systems estimate sigma_b to
    about +/-20%.
  DECISION_RULE: Proceed to E3 only if checks (a) to (f) pass. If ERB fails in a class, that class's primary endpoint reverts to realized truth with at least 8 replicates and S is recomputed. If the CRN variance-reduction factor
    is below 1.2, CRN is kept (no bias) but the study is sized without it. If attribution fails (e), it is used only descriptively. Positive: a validated-measurement methods section and appendix, and the variance components that
    size E5 and E6. Null or negative: no new policy is scored until the failure is fixed and the check rerun.
  PREREQUISITES: E1. Patrick's approval of the harness changes: shared CRN namespaces (lifting paired_episode.py:114-117), an evaluator-only conjugate posterior with prior means, in-process numerical policies, more than 8 policies
    per roster. A written protocol.
  COST: About 5 to 30 CPU core-hours on the local Mac. The range reflects unmeasured in-process TR latency; TR+ and plan-once dominate. Code and review: 1.5 to 3 weeks (new protocol and binding modules, noise keying in world.py,
    the evaluator, the analysis package). No GPU.
  MAIN_RISK: New harness code has historically produced new guard failures. In-process latency may exceed assumptions, which delays the timeline.

### E3 [must] Headroom and identifiability audit of candidate world classes (numerical references only)
  QUESTION: In which candidate classes can a public-information policy beat TR by a detectable margin? In which is use of feedback identifiable from outcomes? What effect size and number of systems follow?
  DESIGN: Classes: C0, the dev generator, as a negative control. Plus at least 2 method-panel classes, each with TR's handicap stated before the audit (for example partial-measurement actions with cost ratios of at least 4; text
    hints with a known conditional law and 2 initial observations; a stronger prior shift). Per class: 48 new calibration systems, R=2 (R=1 for the non-myopic and clairvoyant arms). Arms: TR-32; TR+; LI; coverage; random; plan-
    once with local search; a static schedule tuned leave-one-system-out on training-generator systems by graph x menu; a non-myopic reference (two-step lookahead or rollout TR); an informed reference (TR planning with the
    generating prior or with text mapped by a fixed rule, prediction by the shared learner); a clairvoyant design bound using the true parameters. ERB conditions every arm on the same system-level public information plus its own
    data. Endpoint: the registered band cost (per-system SD units and natural units). Headrooms: H_attain = TR_primary - best attainable reference; H_ceiling = TR - clairvoyant; H_feedback = plan-once - TR; H_state = static
    schedule - TR. Sample size basis: 48 systems give a headroom SE of about 0.144 SD units, so a 0.3 headroom is separated from 0 at the 80% one-sided bound.
  DECISION_RULE: Registered before E3 runs, with no LLM involved. A class qualifies if all lower 80% bounds hold: H_ceiling >= 0.6, H_attain >= 0.3, H_feedback >= 0.15, H_state >= 0.3 SD units. Qualifiers are chosen in a pre-
    declared order. C0 is expected to fail, which validates the audit. The class, delta (default the larger of 0.3 SD units and half of H_attain, with a minimal important difference of 5% of TR's mean band cost), sigma-hat and S
    are frozen here. Positive: the class is frozen, and the paper reports the whole headroom table including classes that failed. Null (no class qualifies): no superiority confirmation is run. The method panel may propose new
    classes by LLM-independent criteria for at most 2 rounds; then the program moves to the null/diagnostic paper with T4 as the explanation. Negative (small H_ceiling everywhere): a theorem-backed statement that tractable known-
    graph classes leave no room for any policy.
  PREREQUISITES: E2 passed. Class modules from the method panel (protocol modules; evaluator posterior with an informed prior; text generator with a known law). Lead approval of CPU time beyond the local Mac if needed.
  COST: About 15 to 60 CPU core-hours across 3 to 4 classes, dominated by the non-myopic and clairvoyant arms. Local Mac or CURC CPU. 1 to 2 weeks including class modules. No GPU.
  MAIN_RISK: T4 predicts that classes whose risk depends only on the design will fail, so every candidate may fail. A weak non-myopic reference understates H_attain; H_ceiling brackets it.

### E4 [must] Treatment-fidelity standard, leakage audit and pre-scoring gates
  QUESTION: What exactly does an LLM arm decide with? Does each candidate pass pre-declared fidelity, learnability and leakage gates before any outcome is scored?
  DESIGN: (a) Retrospective: forward-only scoring of the base, SFT and DPO adapters on all 1,858 saved prompts (1,698 deployment + 160 training) over all legal commands. Compute full-vocabulary and grammar-renormalized log-
    probabilities, whole-command argmax against greedy trie decoding, and length normalization. Score 300 stratified states x 4 menu permutations and identifier renamings. Check the runtime against the saved CURC scores (2 x 1,184
    legal-command scores, 238 reference scores). Metrics: decision-rule disagreement rate; between-state share of centered command-score variance; mutual information between action and state features; invariance rate; agreement
    with TR-256; out-of-sample label NLL against the state-blind counter (1.93 nats). (b) Prospective gates for any candidate on validation systems: G1, score vectors logged for 100% of decisions; G2, at least 90% of decisions
    unchanged under menu permutation when the top-2 margin exceeds 0.5 nats; G3, state-permuted inference changes at least 20% of decisions and the MI permutation test gives p < 0.01; G4, leave-world-out label NLL at most the
    counter minus 0.1 nats; G5, disjoint namespace hashes, no private fields in prompts, equal hyperparameter budgets for all learned arms on validation data only. Sample size: all 1,858 prompts; the invariance rate is estimated
    to +/-2.9 points on 300 states.
  DECISION_RULE: The decision rule for all future LLM arms is frozen on a priori grounds (TWOSOME, trie-mass pooling), not chosen from outcomes: grammar-renormalized, length-normalized whole-command argmax with randomized menu
    order and logged score vectors. A candidate failing any of G1 to G5 is not promoted, and the failure is reported. If the local runtime deviates from the saved CURC scores by more than 1e-3 nats, run on CURC instead. Positive:
    a treatment-fidelity section and a score-level account of the dev collapse (how many decisions flip under whole-command argmax). Null (the metrics fail to discriminate base, SFT and DPO): revise the metrics before E5.
    Negative: runtime mismatch, handled by moving to CURC.
  PREREQUISITES: Patrick's go-ahead for local forward-only inference under a written protocol, or for CURC GPU time. No training.
  COST: About 1 to 2 hours on the local M5 Max (not measured), or 0.5 to 1.2 GPU-slice-hours on CURC (ledger basis: 0.12 to 0.4 s per command forward). 2 to 3 analyst days.
  MAIN_RISK: Local torch and transformers versions differ from the frozen runtime. Mitigated by the saved-score equivalence check.

### E5 [must] Validation screen with registered promotion and futility rules
  QUESTION: Does any candidate recipe, developed only on training and validation namespaces, show enough benefit and contribution to justify a confirmation?
  DESIGN: A fresh 48-system validation shard from the qualified class for each iteration, R=1 with CRN, at most 3 iterations. Candidates: at most 3 recipes from the method panel x K=3 training seeds. Arms: the candidates;
    TR_primary; the best numerical reference from E3; the tuned static schedule; a numerical student trained on the same data with the same tuning budget (K=3); the frozen base LLM with the frozen decision rule; for the leading
    candidate, inference-time feedback-shuffled and state-permuted controls. Zero-cost pre-screens prune candidates before full episodes: first-decision quality at the shared state, and TR-256 relative regret on validation states
    (futility only, never a claim). Endpoint: the registered band cost. Sample size basis: 48 systems give an SE of 0.144 SD units. The rule promotes a candidate with true d=-0.3 with probability 0.85 and one with d=0 with
    probability 0.15; the best of 3 under the global null is promoted with probability 0.38. That costs compute, not alpha, because the confirmation is separate. The SD of the LLM-vs-TR contrast estimated here (upper 80% limit)
    sizes E6.
  DECISION_RULE: Promote the best seed-averaged candidate only if all hold: (i) gates G1 to G5 pass for at least 2 of 3 seeds; (ii) d-hat vs TR <= -0.15 and the one-sided 80% upper bound is below 0; (iii) d-hat <= -0.15 against
    both the shuffled-feedback and the state-permuted controls. If the candidate fails only against the numerical student, it may be promoted, but the claim that language contributes is registered as exploratory. Futility: no
    promotion after 3 iterations stops LLM development. Positive: the recipe is frozen, K=5 seeds are trained on the training namespace, and E6 to E9 are registered. Null or negative: Patrick decides whether to run E6 in null mode
    (best candidate, TOST and harm hypotheses) or to write the null paper. Every candidate and iteration goes into the failure ledger.
  PREREQUISITES: E3 (class, delta). E4 gates. Method-panel recipes. A new compute ceiling. Training namespace distinct from validation, calibration and confirmation.
  COST: About 3.1 GPU-slice-hours of inference per iteration (0.6 s per decision, 24 decisions per episode, no reasoning tokens; multiply by 10 to 50 if the recipe uses reasoning), plus training of 9 policies per iteration
    (recipe-dependent; the current recipe took 291.5 s per run). 2 to 5 CPU core-hours.
  MAIN_RISK: Forking paths across iterations, mitigated by fresh shards and the cap of 3. Large seed variance may block promotion even for a recipe that sometimes works.

### E6 [must] Registered confirmation on fresh systems
  QUESTION: Does the frozen recipe reach a Bayes-reliable answer at lower expected experimental cost than TR on fresh systems (H1)? How does it compare with the strongest numerical reference (H2)?
  DESIGN: Confirmation namespace (fresh keyed scope, system IDs and seeds hashed in the registration). 96 systems stratified equally over the class strata. R=1 with CRN for all systems plus a second replicate on a random 24
    (variance components). Arms: the LLM recipe x 5 seeds; TR_primary; TR-32 (continuity); the best numerical reference from E3; local information; coverage; random; the tuned static schedule; plan-once; the numerical student x 5
    seeds (the standing ranker control under the new recipe); the frozen base LLM; the supervised-only stage x 2 seeds; if the recipe has a stop action, an LLM with stop authority as a secondary arm. Primary endpoint: band cost to
    ERB <= kappa*rho0 averaged over log-uniform kappa in [kappa_lo, 1/2], capped at the budget. Per system it is averaged over seeds and replicates. Key secondary endpoints: normalized Bayes risk at the budget (ratio of arithmetic
    means, valid under T1); reliability at the public stop (ERB and realized truth); cost to an absolute 0.04 target, with systems at rho0 <= 0.04 excluded by a pre-policy rule. Descriptive: the declared first-crossing endpoint,
    realized-truth curves, TR-256 regret, fidelity metrics, and attribution at disagreement states. Analysis is blinded. Sample size basis: superiority at one-sided 0.025 with 80% power and d=0.3 needs 89; TOST at +/-0.3 needs 95;
    so S=96. On dev planning SDs, d=0.3 is about 3 to 9% lower expected error, or at most 0.4 to 1.0 band-cost units. Registered sizing rule: S = max(48, min(150, formula)), using sigma-hat from E5 and delta from E3.
  DECISION_RULE: H1: superiority, one-sided alpha 0.025, by a stratified studentized sign-flip test on per-system contrasts, with a BCa interval. If H1 is rejected, H2 (non-inferiority at delta, then superiority, against the best
    numerical reference) and the E7 family are tested by Holm. Seeds: at least 4 of 5 seeds individually favorable, plus a Hartung-Knapp random-effects interval reported. Positive (H1 rejected and at least 4 of 5 seeds favorable):
    claim a benefit over TR in class C with its interval; H2 decides between 'matches or beats the best numerical reference' and 'beats myopic TR only'. Null (H1 not rejected and TOST passes at +/-delta): 'no benefit larger than
    delta'; this leads to the null paper. No replacement class is allowed. A method redirect needs a new registration and namespace and must report this confirmation. Negative (95% interval adverse): report harm and narrow the
    claim. Inconclusive: report it as such, with no post hoc extension of S.
  PREREQUISITES: E2 and E3 passed. Promotion in E5, or Patrick's null-mode decision. Registration frozen (class, namespaces, artifact hashes, decision rule, endpoints, hypotheses, S/R/K, failure treatment, analysis code hash). A
    new compute ceiling. Custody sharding and raised roster limits (more than 8 policies, more than 64 worlds). Confirmation scope lifted in three modules.
  COST: About 3.6 GPU-slice-hours of LLM inference, plus 20% for failures. 5 to 25 CPU core-hours for the numerical arms (in-process versus the current per-decision-process harness, at 47.7 s per episode). Training of 5 recipe
    seeds plus 2 supervised-only seeds (recipe-dependent). 1 to 2 weeks with custody.
  MAIN_RISK: No candidate is promoted. Heterogeneity across training seeds. Custody and harness failures at a scale never run before.

### E7 [must] Contribution-isolation controls
  QUESTION: Is any LLM effect attributable to using measurements, causal structure and language, rather than to a schedule, extra computation, an identifier or label prior, or narration of planner statistics?
  DESIGN: On the E6 confirmation systems with the same CRN, using pre-registered seeds 1 and 2. c1, feedback-shuffled: post-initialization responses are replaced with coherent decoy responses from another system of the same
    stratum and the posterior summaries are recomputed (96 systems). c2, state-permuted: the whole public state comes from another system at the same step (96). c3, menu-order and identifier permutation (48; equivalence). c4, a
    graph-ablated policy trained and evaluated without the graph text (48; 2 seeds). c5, semantic class only: a metadata ladder of anonymized, correct, irrelevant and misleading metadata, plus misleading x shuffled feedback (48).
    Its endpoint adds recovery cost: the first cost at which the misleading arm's ERB path comes within 10% of the anonymized arm's. c6, the semantic-feature numerical student from E6 (the same information, including frozen-LLM
    text embeddings, trained on the same data). c7, compute-matched TR: draws or lookahead matching the LLM's per-decision wall-clock on the same hardware (96, CPU). c8, an argmax-of-input baseline if any planner statistic appears
    in the prompt. c9, the open-loop bound: the best static design from the post-initialization state by local search (96, CPU). Sample size basis: at S=96 the Holm worst step (one-sided 0.005) detects d=0.35, and at 48 systems
    d=0.49. By T5, if H1 holds then c1's effect is at least H_feedback + d_H1, about 0.45 or more under the E3 gate (approximate, because plan-once upper-bounds the open-loop optimum).
  DECISION_RULE: Second-level family, tested only if H1 is rejected: Holm at one-sided 0.025 over c1, c2, c6, c7 and c9, plus c5 (correct vs anonymized) in a semantic class. c3 is tested by TOST at +/-delta/2. If H1 fails, all
    controls are reported with intervals as diagnostics for the null paper. c1 or c2 equivalent to the LLM: the benefit does not come from using measurements; the claim narrows to a prior or schedule effect. c6 equivalent:
    language adds nothing beyond its features. c7 equivalent or better: the benefit is extra computation. c9 not beaten: no outcome-based certificate of feedback use. c3 not equivalent: the policy depends on identifiers or menu
    position (a fidelity failure, disclosed). c5 recovery cost above half the budget: a robustness limitation, stated in the abstract.
  PREREQUISITES: E6 registration (the controls are registered with it). Training of the graph-ablated policy (2 seeds). For c5, a semantic class qualified in E3.
  COST: About 2.3 GPU-slice-hours of inference, plus 1.9 for the semantic ladder, plus 2 short training runs for c4. 3 to 10 CPU core-hours for c7 and c9.
  MAIN_RISK: Decoy prompts may be out of distribution and degrade the policy for reasons other than feedback use. Mitigated by coherent, same-stratum decoys with recomputed summaries.

### E8 [should] Transfer beyond the training generator (synthetic families)
  QUESTION: Does the frozen policy's effect, or its null, hold on system families outside the training generator?
  DESIGN: Two confirmatory families and one exploratory family, registered with E6 before unblinding and chosen by LLM-independent criteria: F1, graphs of 4 to 6 nodes within the 8-node harness limit; F2, different query laws and
    cost ratios; F3 (exploratory), nonlinear truth with the linear learner, if world.py is extended. 72 systems per confirmatory family, R=1 with CRN. Arms: the LLM (seeds 1 to 3), TR_primary, the best numerical reference, LI,
    random, and the numerical student (seeds 1 to 3). Endpoint: band cost on ERB, or realized truth with R=8 where the law is not computable. Sample size basis: Holm over 2 families at one-sided 0.025 detects d=0.36 per family at
    80% power.
  DECISION_RULE: Transfer is claimed for a family if its LLM-minus-TR contrast is negative at the Holm-adjusted one-sided 0.025 level. Whether it retains at least 50% of the in-class effect is reported descriptively. Positive:
    'transfers to family F'. Null: the claim's scope is limited to the training generator. Negative: harm outside the generator, stated in the abstract. No family may be added or dropped after unblinding.
  PREREQUISITES: E6 registration. Raised harness limits for node counts, menu sizes and roster. F3 needs nonlinear mechanisms in world.py (medium) and realized-truth replicates.
  COST: About 2.6 GPU-slice-hours (prompts about 1.5x longer for larger graphs), plus about 1 for F3. 3 to 10 CPU core-hours. About 1 week, in parallel with E6.
  MAIN_RISK: Prompt length and latency grow with graph size. Family modules take medium effort.

### E9 [should] Concrete experimentalist task: wind-tunnel simulator, then the real Remote Lab if approved
  QUESTION: Does the frozen policy give a useful, observable outcome at lower cost on a physical-style task with real costs and model misspecification?
  DESIGN: Stage A, offline: the causalchamber wind-tunnel simulators (affinity-law fan speed, cubic current, squared-speed pressure; numpy only) wrapped as a response adapter with time-based costs (a load change about 10 s
    including 8 s of settling; repeat measurements cheaper). Task: predict downwind pressure at registered operating points with the knowingly misspecified linear SCM learner. 48 simulator instances with parameters randomized
    within documented ranges plus barometer error, R=4. Arms: the LLM (seeds 1 to 3), TR_primary, LI, random, and the best reference. Endpoints: realized error against the noise-free simulator on the query grid at fixed cost; cost
    at the public stop plus truth reliability at stop. ERB is not available under misspecification. Stage B, hardware: one Wind Tunnel Mk2; 4 policies (LLM seed 1, TR_primary, LI, random) x 30 randomized blocks x 16 interventions;
    a validation grid measured with replicates at the start and end of each session for drift; block-paired mixed-model analysis. Sample size basis: Stage A detects d=0.40 (two-sided 0.05, 80%); Stage B detects 0.51 within-
    apparatus SD units. One apparatus means no population inference.
  DECISION_RULE: Stage B runs if Stage A passes engineering qualification (latency, legality, cost accounting, drift protocol), regardless of how the policies performed. Positive (non-inferior error at lower cost in Stage A, same
    direction in Stage B): 'a useful outcome and transfer on one apparatus'. Null: no practical benefit shown, and the paper says so. Negative: harm on the physical task is reported prominently. Results are never pooled with the
    synthetic confirmation.
  PREREQUISITES: Patrick's approval to pip install causalchamber (Stage A). For Stage B, Remote Lab sign-up, terms acceptance and spend, all by a human. Frozen policies from the E6 registration.
  COST: Stage A: 1 to 2 weeks of integration, about 1.5 GPU-slice-hours, under 5 CPU core-hours. Stage B: about 5.3 hours of chamber time, about 270 EUR at the academic rate (up to about 700 EUR with pilots), 1 to 2 weeks.
  MAIN_RISK: The fidelity of the Mk1 simulators to Mk2 hardware is undocumented. Drift and ambient pressure. One apparatus.

### E10 [should] Blinded independent re-analysis and computational reproduction
  QUESTION: Can every registered number be reproduced by an independent analyst from the sealed outputs and the registration alone, and is the pipeline deterministic?
  DESIGN: (a) Arm codes stay masked until the analysis code is frozen and its hash registered. (b) An analyst not involved in method development (ideally the coauthor) re-implements the primary endpoint, tests and intervals from
    the registration text and compares the results with the frozen code. (c) A random 10% of confirmation cells (about 10 systems x all arms) is re-executed and checked for episode equality (bitwise, or ERB within 1e-9). (d) A
    failure ledger lists every recipe, iteration, crash and exclusion. (e) A superseding statement addresses the public 70% DPO claim. Sample size basis: re-executing 10% of cells detects nondeterminism that affects at least 30%
    of cells with probability above 0.97.
  DECISION_RULE: Any discrepancy beyond tolerance blocks submission until it is resolved and documented. Positive: a reproducibility statement and an archive. Negative: fix, re-report, and disclose the discrepancy.
  PREREQUISITES: E6 sealed outputs and registration. An independent analyst named by Patrick.
  COST: About 0.4 GPU-slice-hours, under 2 CPU core-hours, 2 to 3 analyst days.
  MAIN_RISK: A reviewer from inside the project is not fully independent, which must be disclosed.

## THEORY_ITEMS
- T1, Rao-Blackwellized evaluation. Setting: Pi is the generating law of (theta, side information I), F_t the policy's information, f-hat_t any F_t-measurable predictor. Claim: for every policy that acts on F only and every
  F-stopping time tau, E[loss(theta, f-hat_tau)] = E[rho_tau] with rho_tau = E_Pi[loss | F_tau], and Var(rho_tau) <= Var(loss_tau). Corollary (a): when Pi equals the learner's prior and I is trivial, rho is the deployable
  posterior risk, and the public stop rule rho <= c guarantees expected error <= c on attainment for any acquisition policy. Corollary (b): when I is informative, the learner's posterior risk is biased and can be gamed. Explicit
  counterexample: a policy that skips measurement when the hints indicate a large |theta|. The evaluator's rho, conditioned on I, stays unbiased. Corollary (c): conditioning on both arms' information jointly gives a contrast with
  variance no larger than the raw contrast's. Why it strengthens the paper: it answers the circularity objection to model-based endpoints, states exactly when posterior risk may and may not be used, and justifies a reduction of 8
  to 96 times in the systems needed.
- T2, threshold-averaging identity. For any error path with running minimum m(c), the mean first-crossing cost over a log-uniform band of thresholds, censored at the budget, equals c0 plus a clipped log-area under ln m(c). The
  declared single-threshold endpoint is the degenerate band. Why it strengthens the paper: it proves the repaired primary endpoint generalizes the declared one rather than replacing it, removes the arbitrariness of the 0.04
  threshold, and keeps the endpoint interpretable as cost.
- T3, paired-branch performance difference. J(pi) - J(TR) = E_pi[sum_t A^TR(s_t, a_t)], with the advantage exactly zero wherever pi agrees with TR. The common-random-number branch estimator is unbiased, with a variance bound. Why
  it strengthens the paper: it attributes outcomes to individual decisions, anchored to truth rather than to TR's score, and gives a formal test of whether the LLM contributes or narrates.
- T4, adaptivity gap in conjugate, known-graph linear-Gaussian classes. If every design row entering the target is deterministic under every action and the target is linear in the parameters, the optimal adaptive Bayes risk equals
  the optimal static design's (Chaloner-Verdinelli). With composed (bilinear) queries and random rows, the gap is bounded by the terms that depend on the means and rows (mu_phi' V mu_phi, m' C_phi m). Why it strengthens the paper:
  it explains why the dev class left no room for any learned policy, and it turns world-class selection into a criterion that does not depend on LLM performance, which respects the no-favorable-replacement rule.
- T5, open-loop lower bound. Any policy whose actions are conditionally independent of post-initialization measurements given the initial information has, for any endpoint that is an expectation, a risk at least the open-loop
  optimum R*_OL(F0). Why it strengthens the paper: beating R*_OL is an outcome-based certificate of feedback use, the shuffled-feedback control is identifiable only where TR is below R*_OL, and it gives the power argument for that
  control (an effect of at least H_feedback + d_H1).
- T6, short lemma on decoding. Greedy decoding over a prefix trie of command strings equals the whole-command argmax only if, at every branch, the subtree with the most pooled mass contains the most probable leaf. Seven 'set_*'
  commands sharing a prefix against one 'observe' violates this. Why it strengthens the paper: it formalizes why the treatment definition must be frozen as whole-command scoring with randomized menus, and it explains part of the
  dev arms' convergence on set_x_neg.

## WHAT_NOT_TO_DO
- Do not use first crossing of realized NMSE at a fixed absolute threshold as the primary endpoint. At n=24 its detectable difference is 3.7 units, above the 3.04 headroom, and a 1-unit difference needs about 300 systems.
- Do not use the learner's posterior risk or the TR-score regret as a confirmatory endpoint in classes with side information or prior misspecification. They are biased or circular there (T1). Use them only as screens and
  diagnostics, or where the validity conditions of T1 hold.
- Do not scale up the number of systems before the measurement repairs. Realized-error endpoints need about 1,000 to 2,000 single-episode systems for a 10% contrast.
- Do not run or tune any new policy on the 24 development systems, and do not reuse a validation shard across method iterations.
- Do not choose or change the world class after seeing LLM results, add transfer families after unblinding, or extend S after the fact. A failed confirmation is reported, never replaced by a friendlier benchmark.
- Do not drop systems after scoring. Only the registered pre-policy rule may exclude systems.
- Do not let fallbacks, fills or caps mask policy failures. Score them as no further acquisitions and log them.
- Do not compare a tuned LLM against untuned numerical controls. Every learned arm gets the same tuning budget on validation data only.
- Do not present single-seed LLM results as evidence about the recipe.
- Do not put planner statistics in the prompt without the argmax-of-input baseline. Without it, narration cannot be told apart from contribution.
- Do not evaluate new LLM arms with greedy trie decoding, or with a fixed menu order and fixed identifiers.
- Do not claim the LLM uses feedback in a class where plan-once ties TR. The claim is unidentifiable from outcomes there (T5).
- Do not run a confirmation in a class that failed the E3 headroom gate. It would be underpowered by construction.

## BIGGEST_UNCERTAINTIES
- Whether any tractable class has headroom over TR that survives the informed and non-myopic numerical references. T4 predicts that classes whose risk depends only on the design do not.
- The true per-system SD of LLM-versus-TR contrasts under CRN and ERB. Dev numerical contrasts suggest 0.10 to 0.39 log units, but the right S could lie anywhere from 48 to 150.
- Training-seed variance of new recipes. If it is large, K=5 seeds give little power for recipe-level claims, and promotion in E5 may fail even for a recipe that sometimes works.
- In-process computational cost of TR, TR+, the non-myopic reference and the clairvoyant bound. These are unmeasured, and they drive E2 and E3 CPU between about 5 and 60 core-hours.
- LLM inference cost if the recipe uses reasoning tokens. It could be 10 to 50 times the 0.6 s per decision basis, multiplying every GPU estimate.
- Whether ERB can be computed for semantic-hint and misspecified classes without heavy Monte Carlo. Under physical misspecification (E9) the fallback is realized truth with low power.
- Whether the harness changes (CRN keying, evaluator posteriors, raised roster limits, sharded custody) introduce new guard failures, as harness changes have before.
- The venue and date, which decide how much of the program fits before submission.
