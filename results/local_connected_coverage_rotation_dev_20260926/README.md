# Coverage-order audit on connected motifs

This **post hoc development diagnostic** revisits the corrected connected-motif systems, seeds 200–202, N=30, k=3 or 10, root SD .15, actuator penalty 4, and cost budget 400. It tests every cyclic start of the deterministic coverage schedule: three rotations at k=3 and ten at k=10. The other three policies, including posterior-risk pairs, retain their original decisions and random streams. Offset zero reproduces the archived action logs exactly and each metric within 1e−10. The 195 arm metrics, 1,365 actions, 13 rotation summaries, configuration, and SHA-256 receipt are preserved here; hashes, unique cells, finite outcomes, and fixed risk actions across rotations validate.

With k=10, the original coverage-pair controller has only five actions and visits motifs 0–4. Rotating its start changes which five motifs receive paired actions. Mean final feasible-motif MSE over the three systems ranges from **0.13774 to 0.41681** across rotations; risk pairs remain **0.04211** and beat coverage pairs in all **30 system×rotation comparisons**. Thus fixed starting order does not explain the risk-pair advantage over this particular rotating-coverage control at k=10. Coverage singles, with ten actions and more environment samples at the same cost, range from 0.05125 to 0.11859 across starts; risk pairs are below their three-system means here, but the sample is small.

With k=3, coverage-pair means range from **0.02210 to 0.16209**, while risk pairs remain **0.04720**. Risk wins only 5/9 system×rotation comparisons. The original offset zero is the strongest tested coverage rotation on average. This reinforces that the within-menu selector result is conditional on motif count and coverage ordering.

These are rotations on the **same three development SCMs**, not thirteen independent systems per setting. Risk uses an exact linear posterior, known DAG, and student-simulated candidate contexts; no foundation model or neural mechanism is involved. The k=10 result motivates a separately frozen test against stronger order-independent controls, including balanced/randomized motif allocation under the same pair menu, on new graph structures. It does not overcome the prior promotion failure at k=3. Do not pool rotations as independent replicates or submit a large confirmation grid yet.
