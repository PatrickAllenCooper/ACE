# Fresh coverage-floor transfer study (29 September 2026)

The [protocol](../../docs/development/guidance/protocol_transfer_floor6_fresh_2026-09-29.json) and runner were frozen at `e17e69d11e8d4bdaead9ca798083b2ee44c737ae` before generating 20 new systems (1300–1319). Each system has 30 modules and one changed target mechanism. Source modules are estimated from 16 or 64 counted observations per node. Both target settings add a form absent from the fitted six-feature bank: `0.85*tanh(1.7*x1+0.8*x2)` (strong) or `0.45*tanh(1.7*x1+0.8*x2)` (weak). The independent 1,024-context evaluator scores the full true conditional mean.

After four target responses per node, the candidate gives two more to every node, then five more to each of the top four source-versus-scratch evidence-ranked nodes. It spends **200** target responses per policy, as do the uniform and previous top-eight controls. This six-response floor was chosen after the prior 20-system allocation-harm audit; the new systems were not used to choose it.

**All 16 prespecified point contrasts pass.** The floor policy's soft-mixture changed-node MSE divided by uniform soft-mixture MSE is **0.1977/0.1352** for strong/weak changes with 16 source responses, and **0.1977/0.1156** with 64 source responses (limits 0.8). Its unchanged-node ratios are **1.0301/1.0298** at source size 16 and **0.9983/0.9983** at source size 64 (limits 1.05). It also passes the changed-node comparison against its own scratch fit and unchanged-node comparison against its own source-warm fit. In absolute terms, the 16-source unchanged-node mean error is about 0.01005 versus 0.00975 under uniform. Its paired interval for the difference is positive, so the point gate passing must not be read as no harm. The paired intervals for changed-node benefit versus uniform include zero; the point gate does not establish a population-level improvement.

The previous top-eight policy has lower changed-node error than the floor policy on these systems, but its earlier unchanged-node protection failure motivated the floor. The changed node falls in the top four for **20/20 strong systems** at both source sizes and **19/20 weak systems** at source size 16 (20/20 at 64). Localization remains easy. The held-out term has mean noise-free six-feature projection residual MSE **0.04551** (strong) or **0.01276** (weak). For strong changes, the floor policy's mixture prediction is numerically the scratch fit on the changed node; it does not demonstrate a special reusable-source advantage. On untouched nodes it is effectively source-warm. This is a numerical coverage/acquisition result within the synthetic generator, not a successful modular-transfer or foundation-model result.

Custody: **40 source receipts**, **80 target receipts**, **48,000 source training responses**, **48,000 target arm-response counts**, **20,704** within-cell union prefixes summed across cells, and **11,064** global unique acquired response identities across source-size arms and settings. All 21,600 node-method scores were independently recomputed from stored acquired responses and source posteriors; file hashes, allocation rules, exact budgets, evaluation hashes, and finite outputs validated. A full rerun was byte-identical. NumPy emitted matrix-operation warnings on this host, but every score was finite and independent contractions reproduced the stored results. The study ran locally in seconds; no CURC job or closed-model API was used.

The next scientific gate is a harder change-localization distribution or different mechanism/graph family, with an explicitly source-aware predictor that improves on adaptive scratch. Repeating this same synthetic family would not establish that gain.
