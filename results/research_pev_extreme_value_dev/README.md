# Endpoint-value control: development outcome

Six new CURC runs on 27 September 2026 complete the [prespecified three-system diagnostic](../../docs/development/guidance/protocol_pev_extreme_value_control_dev.md). They reuse shift30 seeds 42, 123, and 456 and compare with twelve archived runs on those same systems. This is exploratory development evidence: the endpoint-value hypothesis was chosen after inspecting prior action logs, and these are not fresh confirmation systems.

The final noise-free feasible nonroot MSE means, each over three paired systems, are: ordinary graph-aware coverage 0.018849, ordinary graph-aware random 0.020677, PEV 0.011167, naive-variance PEV 0.010556, **endpoint coverage 0.008802**, and **endpoint random 0.034605**. Lower is better. Endpoint coverage beats PEV on all three systems (paired differences −0.002042, −0.003480, −0.001575) and also beats naive-variance PEV on all three. Endpoint random is worse on average, driven particularly by seed 123 (0.078066), so extreme values alone are insufficient; the target schedule matters. Ordinary coverage selected mean absolute values 2.573, PEV 4.543, naive variance 4.737, and both endpoint controls exactly 5.000. See `comparison.csv` for every seed, metric, action magnitude, and target count.

The action-value confound explains at least part of the previous advantage over coverage on these systems. It does not prove that endpoint coverage will beat PEV on fresh systems or a different graph/function generator, nor does it isolate the integrated-variance formula from naive variance. A future independent confirmation should freeze endpoint coverage as a primary baseline and score final noise-free feasible nonroot MSE, with action values logged. Do not promote PEV's earlier gain over ordinary coverage as an uncertainty-specific result.
